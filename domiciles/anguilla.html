Anguilla

Anguilla licenses insurers under the Insurance Act, R.S.A. c. I16, administered by the Anguilla Financial Services Commission. Act 6/2004 came into force on 1 October 2004. The revised edition published by the Commission shows the law as at 15 December 2014 and consolidates Act 6/2004, Act 3/2006, and Act 4/2013. The dollar in the Act is a United States dollar. Class B licences are the offshore and captive classes. Issued and paid-up capital is at least $200,000 for an unrestricted Class B licence, $100,000 for a general Class B licence, $100,000 for an association Class B licence, $25,000 for a group Class B licence, and $10,000 for a single Class B licence. The Commission may order a higher amount. The Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2018, Act No. 6/2018, added a producer affiliated reinsurance company licence. That licence is restricted to reinsurance and requires issued and paid-up capital of at least $5,000. Act No. 10/2018 replaced the definition so that the ultimate beneficial owners are the same as those of, or affiliated with, the producer. The Commission's Director's Report for 2023 states that 47 captive insurers held licences at 31 December 2023, that no new captive licences were granted in 2023, and that there were 15 licence cancellations, down from 62 at the beginning of 2023. The report does not split that count by licence class, and no later annual report is published on the reports page. The Insurance (Amendment) Regulations, 2018, set the current fees. A Class B application is $1,500 for an unrestricted licence, $1,000 for a general licence, and $750 for an association, group, or single licence or for a producer affiliated reinsurance company. The annual licence fee is $4,000 for an unrestricted licence carrying on general and long-term business, $3,000 for an unrestricted long-term licence, $3,000 for a general licence, $2,500 for an association, group, or single licence, and $750 for a producer affiliated reinsurance company in each calendar year after the year of grant. Section 6 requires the annual fee by 15 January. The Act does not impose a premium tax. Section 18(4) exempts contracts executed for the foreign insurance business of a licensed insurer from stamp duty. A fast-track process published by the Commission licenses a captive or producer affiliated reinsurance company within one business day of a complete submission by an approved insurance manager, provided the company has been incorporated. No ordinary turnaround, staff count, or captive association is published.

Tax Environment
The Insurance Act does not impose a premium tax or an annual premium-tax return. Section 18(4) provides that contracts of insurance executed for or in connection with the foreign insurance business of a licensed insurer are exempt from stamp duty. Section 18(1) provides that a company licensed under the Act is not required to be licensed under the Trades, Businesses, Occupations and Professions Licensing Act. The Commission's Director's Report for 2023 states that the Goods and Services Tax Act, which took effect on 1 July 2022 and introduced taxes on insurance premiums, was amended in late 2023 to repeal and remove taxes applicable to the sale of insurance products effective 7 December 2023. No corporate income-tax rate for a Class B insurer or a producer affiliated reinsurance company is stated in the Insurance Act, the Insurance Regulations, or the Commission's insurance pages reviewed. The regulatory charge that is published is the licence fee in Schedule 6, not a premium-tax rate.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Class B unrestricted Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$200,000 (section 3(1)(b)). The licence permits any foreign insurance business, including long-term foreign insurance business. The Act defines a dollar as a dollar of the United States.
Class B general Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$100,000 (section 3(1)(c)). The licence permits general foreign insurance business and not long-term foreign insurance business.
Class B association Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$100,000 (section 3(1)(d), as amended by Act 3/2006). The insurer has two or more owners. It may carry on no more than 50 percent of its foreign insurance business, measured by net premiums written, or 100 percent of its reinsurance business, with persons who are not owners of the insurer or its affiliates. The Commission's insurance page states the 50 percent net-premium limit and does not restate the reinsurance limb.
Class B group Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$25,000 (section 3(1)(e)). The business is with a single owner, that owner's affiliates, and employees of the owner or its affiliates, and may include long-term foreign insurance business.
Class B single Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$10,000 (section 3(1)(f)). The business is with the sole owner, if a company, and may include long-term foreign insurance business. The Commission's insurance page also describes this licence as available to a trust, for business with the beneficial owners. Section 4 allows the shares of a licensed insurer to be owned by trustees of a trust, including a purpose trust, with the Commission's approval.
Producer affiliated reinsurance company Issued and paid-up capital of at least US$5,000 (Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2018, section 3, inserting section 3(1)(g)). The licence permits foreign insurance business, including long-term foreign insurance business, but only as reinsurance. The Commission's procedure of 6 July 2018 requires proof that the paid-up capital has been deposited in a bank account in the company's name within 30 days after the licence is granted.
Higher capital and solvency margin The Commission may order a licensee to increase its paid-up capital, having regard to the nature and extent of the business (section 3(4)). The insurer must report immediately if its net worth falls below the required paid-up capital (section 3(6)). Section 8(13) requires a solvency margin at least equal to the margin specified in regulations or by the Commission. The Commission's guidance notes made on 1 March 2008 state that, for an insurer other than one carrying on long-term business, the margin is the greater of the section 3(1) paid-up capital and a premium test: 20 percent of net retained annual premium where that premium does not exceed US$5,000,000, or US$1,000,000 plus 10 percent of the excess where it does. For long-term business the notes require at least the section 3(1) paid-up capital. An insurer writing both maintains the aggregate.
Form of capital Section 3(1) requires issued and paid-up capital. The Commission's letter-of-credit guidelines, approved by the Board on 10 December 2013, state that the Commission's preference is cash paid for fully paid-up shares. Pending a review of the Act, the Commission will accept a letter of credit as a component of paid-up capital at the initial licensing of a captive insurer if the letter of credit is irrevocable, is issued for the Commission's benefit by a bank licensed in Anguilla with a short-term debt rating of at least A-1 from Standard & Poor's or P-1 from Moody's, is delivered to the Commission before the licence is issued, and follows the stated form. The guidelines do not treat a letter of credit as issued share capital under the Companies Act.

Regulatory Framework

Insurance Act, R.S.A. c. I16, as amended by the Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2018, Act No. 6/2018, and the Insurance (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2018, Act No. 10/2018, and the Insurance Regulations, R.R.A. I16-1, as amended by the Insurance (Amendment) Regulations, 2018

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Insurance Act, R.S.A. c. I16. Act 6/2004 came into force on 1 October 2004. The revised edition on the Commission website shows the law as at 15 December 2014 and consolidates Act 6/2004, Act 3/2006 in force 27 March 2006, and Act 4/2013 in force 25 September 2013. That edition does not include the 2018 amendments. Classes and minimum issued and paid-up capital are section 3(1). The Commission may order a higher amount under section 3(4). Trustee ownership is section 4. The annual fee date is section 6. Accounts, the insurance-manager requirement, and the solvency margin are section 8. Class B annual returns are section 10(3). A company must have at least two directors under section 13. Stamp-duty exemption for foreign insurance contracts is section 18(4). The Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2018, Act No. 6/2018, was passed by the House of Assembly on 10 July 2018 and assented to on 13 July 2018. It inserts the producer affiliated reinsurance company definition and section 3(1)(g), exempts that company from the audit requirement in section 8(5), and requires its return within three months of financial year-end. The Insurance (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2018, Act No. 10/2018, was passed by the House on 14 September 2018 and replaces the definition. Fees are Schedule 6 of the Insurance Regulations as substituted by the Insurance (Amendment) Regulations, 2018, R.A. 16/2018. Solvency margins in the Commission's guidance notes were made on 1 March 2008 under the power cited in those notes. The letter-of-credit guidelines were approved by the Board on 10 December 2013.

Licensing Speed
The Commission's fast-track procedure for captive insurers and producer affiliated reinsurance companies says the Commission will license the applicant within one business day of receiving a complete submission from an insurance manager approved to use the process, provided the applicant has been incorporated. The submission, including the application fee and licence fee, goes to applications@afsc.ai. The licence is approved in reliance on that submission, and the Commission may suspend or revoke it if the submission does not comply with the licensing guidelines. The Act and the published procedure do not state a number of days for an application that is not on the fast track.
Premium Tax Rate
No premium tax is imposed by the Insurance Act. Foreign insurance contracts of a licensed insurer are exempt from stamp duty under section 18(4). The Commission's 2023 Director's Report states that goods and services tax on the sale of insurance products was removed effective 7 December 2023. No corporate income-tax rate for these licensees is published in the Act or the Commission materials reviewed. The standing regulatory charge is the Schedule 6 licence fee.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Schedule 6, as substituted by the Insurance (Amendment) Regulations, 2018, charges a non-refundable application fee of US$1,500 for a Class B licence under section 3(1)(b), US$1,000 for a Class B licence under section 3(1)(c), US$750 for a Class B licence under section 3(1)(d), (e), or (f), and US$750 for a producer affiliated reinsurance company licence under section 3(1)(g). An insurance manager's application fee is US$1,000. The Commission's procedure approved on 6 July 2018 states that the US$750 producer affiliated reinsurance company application fee includes the first year's annual licence fee, and that the company must be incorporated and the fee paid before the licence is granted. Company-incorporation fees payable to the registry are not set in Schedule 6. An insurance manager approved to use the fast-track process pays a separate non-refundable application fee of US$500 and an approval fee of US$2,000, then US$2,000 a year, in addition to its own licence fee.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Section 6 requires every current licensee to pay the prescribed annual fee on or before 15 January. Schedule 6, as substituted in 2018, charges an annual licence fee of US$3,000 for a section 3(1)(b) licence for long-term business, US$4,000 for a section 3(1)(b) licence for general and long-term business, US$3,000 for a section 3(1)(c) general licence, and US$2,500 for a section 3(1)(d), (e), or (f) licence. A producer affiliated reinsurance company pays US$750 for each calendar year following the calendar year in which the licence is granted. If a licence other than that producer affiliated reinsurance company annual charge is granted on or after 1 July, half of the relevant prescribed licence fee is payable at grant. An insurance manager's annual licence fee is US$2,000. The 2018 schedule does not restate the late-payment percentages that appeared in the earlier schedule.
Annual Filing Requirements
A Class B insurer furnishes an annual return within six months of the end of its financial year, including the audited annual accounts required by section 8(5), a certificate of compliance under section 8(4), and such other information as the Commission requires in order to be satisfied of the insurer's solvency and compliance (section 10(3)). Annual accounts are prepared on generally accepted accounting principles acceptable to the Commission and are audited by an independent auditor the Commission approves, unless the Commission exempts the insurer in writing. A producer affiliated reinsurance company is excluded from that audit requirement. It prepares and furnishes, within three months of the end of its financial year, annual accounts in a form acceptable to the Commission, a certificate of compliance, and a solvency calculation in a form acceptable to the Commission. It must obtain the Commission's prior approval for a change in the primary insurer. The Commission's guideline of 30 October 2018 treats a primary insurer as acceptable if it has an A.M. Best financial-strength rating of B+ or higher, or an equivalent rating the Commission accepts, is licensed and supervised in a home jurisdiction the Commission accepts, and is in good standing with its home regulator. The annual licence fee is due by 15 January under section 6. Every insurer other than an approved external insurer, or an insurer that maintains its principal office and staff permanently in Anguilla, appoints an insurance manager and keeps full records at a designated principal office in Anguilla or another location the Commission approves. A company has at least two directors, and a director or senior officer is not appointed without the Commission's prior approval unless the Commission exempts the licensee in writing. A Class B insurer may not carry on domestic business except where that business is a minor part of the international risk of a policyholder whose main activities are outside Anguilla, and it applies to the Commission in writing before doing so. The Act does not state an in-Anguilla board-meeting requirement in the sections reviewed. The Commission publishes licence issuance, revocation, and suspension in the Gazette.
