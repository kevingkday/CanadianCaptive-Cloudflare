Germany

Germany has no separate captive insurance act. A captive is authorized as an insurer or a reinsurer under the Versicherungsaufsichtsgesetz of 1 April 2015, last amended by the Act of 25 September 2026, and is supervised by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht. Section 8 requires an undertaking with its seat in Germany to obtain permission before it carries on business. Permission may be granted to a stock corporation, including a European company, a mutual insurance association, or a public-law corporation or institution. The head office must be in Germany. A reinsurer is authorized only for reinsurance. Directive 2009/138/EC, Article 13, defines a firmeneigenes Versicherungsunternehmen and a firmeneigenes Rückversicherungsunternehmen as an insurer or reinsurer owned by a financial undertaking that is not itself an insurer or reinsurer, or by a non-financial undertaking, and writing only the risks of the owner or of the group. The VAG uses the same captive description in section 45(5), item 10. The absolute floor of the minimum capital requirement is set by section 1(2) of the Kapitalausstattungs-Verordnung: EUR 2.7 million for health and property-casualty business that does not cover classes 10 to 15, including a captive insurer; EUR 4 million if any of those classes is covered; EUR 4 million for life, including a captive insurer; EUR 3.9 million for a reinsurer; and EUR 1.3 million for a captive reinsurer. BaFin's reinsurance-authorization notice of 1 March 2017 still prints the earlier floors of EUR 3.6 million and EUR 1.2 million, and it says only the law in force when the application is made is binding. The fee now in force for an initial permission under section 8(1) is EUR 32,259 under item 19.2 of the Finanzdienstleistungsaufsichtsgebührenverordnung. Corporate income tax is 15 percent through the 2027 assessment year. Insurance premium tax is generally 19 percent, and section 4(1) of the Versicherungsteuergesetz exempts a reinsurance premium. BaFin's 2025 annual report does not publish a captive count. It reported 3,084 employees at 31 December 2025. The organisation chart of 1 October 2026 places captive supervision in Referat VA 46 and does not give that unit's headcount.

Tax Environment
The VAG and the Kapitalausstattungs-Verordnung do not create a captive premium-tax exemption or a captive income-tax exemption. Section 23(1) of the Körperschaftsteuergesetz sets corporate income tax at 15 percent of taxable income for assessment years through 2027, then 14 percent in 2028, 13 percent in 2029, 12 percent in 2030, 11 percent in 2031, and 10 percent from 2032. The solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax were not restated from a captive instrument in this review. Section 6(1) of the Versicherungsteuergesetz sets insurance premium tax at 19 percent of the premium excluding the tax, subject to the special rates in subsection (2), including 22 percent for fire and fire-business-interruption insurance. Section 4(1), item 1, exempts the payment of a reinsurance premium from that tax. A captive that writes direct insurance of risks situated in Germany is therefore in the premium-tax base unless another exemption in section 4 applies. A captive that writes only reinsurance is within the section 4(1) reinsurance exemption. No German equivalent of the French provision pour résilience was found in the statutes reviewed.

How the minimum is set Section 122 of the VAG defines the minimum capital requirement as the amount of eligible basic own funds below which policyholders and claimants would face an unacceptable level of risk if the undertaking continued. Section 1(1) of the Kapitalausstattungs-Verordnung raises a calculated minimum capital requirement that is below 25 percent of the solvency capital requirement up to that 25 percent, and reduces one that is above 45 percent down to 45 percent. A capital add-on under section 301 is included in the solvency capital requirement for that test. Section 1(2) then sets absolute floors that the minimum capital requirement may not go below. Section 89 requires eligible own funds at least equal to the solvency capital requirement, and eligible basic own funds at least equal to the minimum capital requirement. At authorization, section 9 requires basic own funds covering the absolute floor and own funds covering the solvency capital requirement of the expected business.
Health and property-casualty, including a captive insurer, without classes 10 to 15 Absolute floor of EUR 2.7 million where the undertaking does not cover risks in classes 10 to 15 of Annex 1 to the VAG (Kapitalausstattungs-Verordnung, section 1(2), item 1).
Property-casualty, including a captive insurer, covering classes 10 to 15 Absolute floor of EUR 4 million where the undertaking covers a risk in at least one of classes 10 to 15 of Annex 1 (section 1(2), item 2).

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Versicherungsaufsichtsgesetz of 1 April 2015 (BGBl. I p. 434), last amended by Article 3 of the Act of 25 September 2026 (BGBl. 2026 I No. 275). Permission, legal form, and the head-office requirement are section 8. The application and the business plan are section 9. Refusal grounds are section 11. The scope of permission is section 10. The minimum capital requirement is section 122, and its calculation and quarterly report are section 123. The absolute floors are section 1(2) of the Kapitalausstattungs-Verordnung. Own funds covering the solvency capital requirement and the minimum capital requirement are sections 89, 94, and 95. The solvency and financial condition report is section 40. Annual accounts are submitted under section 37. Relief from reporting that is more frequent than annual is section 45, which lists a captive writing only the risks of its industrial or commercial group as one proportionality factor. Withdrawal of permission is section 304. Directive 2009/138/EC, Article 13(2) and Article 13(5), defines the captive insurer and the captive reinsurer. Article 25 requires a judicial remedy if the supervisor has not decided an application within six months of receipt. The initial-permission fee is item 19.2 of the Finanzdienstleistungsaufsichtsgebührenverordnung of 2 September 2021, last amended by Article 4 of the Act of 25 September 2026. Corporate income tax is section 23 of the Körperschaftsteuergesetz. Insurance premium tax is the Versicherungsteuergesetz, sections 4 and 6. BaFin's reinsurance-authorization notice of 1 March 2017 describes the reinsurance application. It still states the pre-indexation floors and the former EUR 10,000 fee.

Licensing Speed
Sections 8 to 11 of the VAG do not state a number of days in which BaFin must grant or refuse an initial permission. Article 25 of Directive 2009/138/EC requires Member States to provide a judicial remedy where the supervisory authority has not decided the application within six months after receiving it. The article requires that remedy. It does not itself require the permission to be granted within six months. BaFin's insurance-authorization page asks applicants to arrange an early preliminary discussion through poststelle@bafin.de. The 2017 reinsurance notice does not state a processing time.
Premium Tax Rate
No captive premium-tax rate is set in the VAG. Versicherungsteuergesetz section 6(1) sets the general rate at 19 percent of the premium excluding the tax. Section 4(1), item 1, exempts a reinsurance premium. Corporate income tax under Körperschaftsteuergesetz section 23(1) is 15 percent through the 2027 assessment year and then declines by one percentage point a year to 10 percent from 2032. No captive exemption from that tax was found.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Item 19.2 of the Finanzdienstleistungsaufsichtsgebührenverordnung charges EUR 32,259 for granting the initial permission to carry on business under section 8(1) of the VAG. The regulation was made on 2 September 2021 and was last amended by Article 4 of the Act of 25 September 2026. The same item also covers certain other initial permissions, including a special purpose vehicle under section 168. BaFin's reinsurance notice of 1 March 2017 states a fee of EUR 10,000 under item 6.2 of the former FinDAGKostV. That notice tells the reader that only the law in force at the time of the application is binding. Commercial-register and notary formation costs are not set in item 19.2.
Annual Maintenance Fee
No captive-specific annual euro amount is set in FinDAGebV item 19. BaFin recovers the cost of ongoing supervision by fees and by allocations charged to supervised undertakings. The allocation formula and the insurance-premium bands were not extracted from the cost-allocation regulation in this review.
Annual Filing Requirements
Section 123 requires the minimum capital requirement to be calculated quarterly and the result reported to BaFin. A quarterly calculation of the solvency capital requirement is not required merely to set the minimum-capital boundaries. If an Article 129(3) boundary determines the minimum capital requirement, the undertaking explains why. Section 45 allows BaFin to exempt an undertaking from reports that delegated acts require more often than once a year, where the cost would be excessive relative to the risks and the information is still submitted at least annually. That exemption cannot remove the quarterly minimum-capital report. One factor in the proportionality test is whether the undertaking is a captive that covers only risks connected with the industrial or commercial group to which it belongs. Section 40 requires a solvency and financial condition report at least annually, no later than 14 weeks after the end of the financial year, approved by the management body before publication and sent to BaFin promptly after publication. The group report has six further weeks. Section 37 requires the annual accounts and the management report, both as drawn up and as adopted, and the audited solvency balance sheet and its audit report, to be submitted to BaFin without delay. Section 135 requires immediate notice if the minimum capital requirement is no longer covered or is expected to be uncovered within three months, and a realistic short-term financing plan within one month. BaFin's 2025 report said 469 insurers, Pensionskassen, and Pensionsfonds were under supervision. That total is not a captive count.
