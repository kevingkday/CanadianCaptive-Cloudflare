Connecticut

Connecticut licenses captive insurance companies under Connecticut General Statutes sections 38a-91aa to 38a-91tt, enacted as Public Act 08-127 and effective 1 January 2009, and administered by the Captive Insurance Division of the Connecticut Insurance Department. The Division's formation page states that the law was amended in 2011, that the Division was established in 2012, and that the first two captives were licensed that year. A captive may be a pure, association, industrial insured, agency, sponsored, special purpose financial, or branch captive, or a risk retention group. A sponsored captive may also be licensed as a special purpose financial captive, and it may establish protected cells and incorporated protected cells. A parametric contract is permitted. A dormant captive, under section 38a-91uu, is a pure, sponsored, or industrial insured captive that has ceased transacting insurance business and has no insurance liabilities. Unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus is the greater of $50,000, or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary, for a pure captive; $75,000 on that basis for a sponsored captive; and $250,000 on that basis for an association, industrial insured, agency, or special purpose financial captive, and for a sponsored captive licensed as a special purpose financial captive. A risk retention group holds at least $1,000,000. A branch captive posts security of the greater of $50,000, or the amount the commissioner determines, plus reserves on branch business. The commissioner may allow any captive other than a risk retention group to hold less. A dormant captive maintains $15,000 and need not add capital on entering dormancy if it was never capitalized. Direct premium tax under section 38a-91nn is 0.380 percent, 0.285 percent, 0.190 percent, and 0.072 percent on successive $20 million bands. Assumed-reinsurance tax is 0.214 percent, 0.143 percent, 0.048 percent, and 0.024 percent. The annual minimum is $7,500 and the annual maximum is $200,000. A nonrefundable $7,500 credit applies in the first year tax is owed. The nonrefundable application fee is $800 and the initial and renewal license fee is $375. Bulletin FS-4C-25, dated 26 December 2025, requires the audited financial report five months after the fiscal year-end and directs filings to CID.CaptiveFilings@ct.gov. The Division pages name Fenhua Liu, Assistant Deputy Commissioner and Director of the Captive Division, and publish an individual mailbox for questions. They do not publish a captive census or a staff count. The application-materials page describes the review steps and does not state a single guaranteed licensing period.

Tax Environment
Section 38a-91nn requires the tax by 1 March, paid to the Commissioner of Revenue Services. Direct premiums, after return premiums and policyholder dividends or premium deposits returned or credited, are taxed at 0.380 percent on the first $20 million, 0.285 percent on the next $20 million, 0.190 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.072 percent thereafter. Annuity considerations are not taxed. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.214 percent, 0.143 percent, 0.048 percent, and 0.024 percent on those bands. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer. For a captive other than a sponsored captive, the annual minimum aggregate tax is $7,500 and the annual maximum aggregate tax is $200,000. Branch tax applies only to branch business. For a sponsored captive, the $7,500 minimum applies to the company as a whole and not to each protected cell. Section 38a-91nn(c)(2) states that the annual maximum tax to be paid by a sponsored captive is the aggregate tax liability, calculated under the direct-premium subsection, of each protected cell. The Department of Revenue Services instructions, last reviewed 15 December 2025, state that the maximum aggregate tax to be paid by a sponsored captive applies to each protected cell and not to the sponsored captive as a whole. Two or more captives under common ownership and control are taxed as one captive. Common ownership and control is 80 percent of the voting stock, of the surplus and voting power of a mutual or nonprofit, or of the membership interests of a limited liability company. A protected cell is treated as a separate captive owned and controlled by its participants. A special purpose financial captive is not consolidated with a captive that is not a special purpose financial captive. The tax is all of the tax collectible under Connecticut law from a captive, and no other occupation tax is levied by the state or by a county, city, or municipality, except sales and use taxes and ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used to produce income. Multiyear premiums are prorated. A captive may claim a nonrefundable credit of $7,500 against the tax for the first calendar year on or after 1 January 2012 in which it has liability under the section. The Division's tax page shortens that credit to a $7,500 credit during the first year of operations. A dormant captive is not liable for the section 38a-91nn tax. The Department of Revenue Services states that the dormant exemption is effective 1 October 2023 and that a certificate of dormancy must be attached to the return. Form 207C is filed and paid on myconneCT by 1 March. A late payment draws the greater of 10 percent of the tax due or $50, plus interest of 1 percent per month or fraction of a month.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive Not less than the greater of $50,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary for the company to meet its policy obligations (Conn. Gen. Stat. section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(A)). The Division's minimum-capital page states USD 50,000.
Association captive Not less than the greater of $250,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(B)).
Industrial insured captive Not less than the greater of $250,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(C)).
Risk retention group Not less than $1,000,000 (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(D)). The discretion to allow less than the statutory floor does not apply to a risk retention group.
Sponsored captive Not less than the greater of $75,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(E)). The Division's minimum-capital page states USD 75,000 for the general account and states that the minimum capital for each cell depends on the risks taken. Section 38a-91dd does not state a separate dollar floor for a protected cell.
Special purpose financial captive Not less than the greater of $250,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(F)).
Sponsored captive licensed as a special purpose financial captive Not less than the greater of $250,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(G)).
Agency captive Not less than the greater of $250,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary (section 38a-91dd(a)(1)(H)).
Branch captive Security for branch liabilities of not less than the greater of $50,000 or an amount the commissioner determines is necessary, plus reserves on branch policies and reinsurance contracts, including losses, allocated loss-adjustment expenses, incurred-but-not-reported losses, and unearned premiums (section 38a-91dd(a)(2)). The commissioner may permit credit against those reserves for qualifying trust assets. The security may be a trust funded with acceptable assets, an irrevocable letter of credit from a bank the commissioner approves, cash on deposit with the commissioner for the base amount only, or a combination. The commissioner may exempt a foreign branch captive that the commissioner determines is financially stable.
Dormant captive Not less than $15,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus. A dormant captive that was never capitalized is not required to add capital on entering dormancy (section 38a-91uu(c)(1)). The Division's minimum-capital page states USD 15,000.
Commissioner discretion and form of capital The commissioner may allow a captive other than a risk retention group to maintain less than the subsection (a) floor, considering the type, volume, and nature of the business (section 38a-91dd(b)). Except for branch security, capital and surplus may be cash or an irrevocable letter of credit issued by a bank the commissioner approves (section 38a-91dd(c)). The commissioner may adopt regulations for additional capital and surplus (section 38a-91dd(d)).

Regulatory Framework

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Connecticut General Statutes sections 38a-91aa to 38a-91tt, originating in Public Act 08-127, effective 1 January 2009, and section 38a-91uu on dormant captives, added by Public Act 17-198. Capital and surplus are section 38a-91dd, revised by Public Act 22-118, effective 1 July 2022. The license and the $800 application fee are section 38a-91bb. The license fee amount is the captive insurance company license in section 38a-11, which the Department states as $375. Annual reports are section 38a-91gg. Examinations are section 38a-91hh, also revised by Public Act 22-118. Premium tax is section 38a-91nn. Protected cells are section 38a-91rr. The 2026 Supplement republishes amended sections 38a-91aa, 38a-91oo, 38a-91rr, and 38a-91zz. Dormancy tax relief was added by Public Act 23-15. Bulletin FS-4C-25 (26 December 2025) states the current financial-filing dates. The Department of Revenue Services captive-tax page, last reviewed 15 December 2025, states the return, the minimum, the maximum, and the dormant exemption.

Licensing Speed
The application-and-regulatory-materials page states that, within the first two business days after filing, the Department assigns an analyst; that the analyst reviews completeness within two business days; that the financial, operational, and risk-profile review is expected to take up to five business days, depending on the risk profile and complexity; that a peer or supervisory review, when needed, is completed within five business days; and that notice of approval or denial is issued immediately after the final supervisory review. The page does not state a single guaranteed number of days from a complete filing to a license. The statute does not state a licensing-day standard. The license, once granted, authorizes business until 1 April.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.380 percent, 0.285 percent, 0.190 percent, and 0.072 percent on successive $20 million bands. Assumed reinsurance: 0.214 percent, 0.143 percent, 0.048 percent, and 0.024 percent on the same bands. Annual minimum $7,500. Annual maximum $200,000. For a sponsored captive the minimum applies to the company as a whole. The Department of Revenue Services states that the sponsored-captive maximum applies to each protected cell. Due 1 March on Form 207C. Nonrefundable $7,500 credit in the first year the tax is owed. A dormant captive pays no section 38a-91nn tax.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 38a-91bb(d) requires a nonrefundable fee of $800 for examining, investigating, and processing the license application. The commissioner may retain outside legal, financial, and examination services and charge the reasonable cost to the company. The Division's premium-tax and fees page states the same one-time initial license application fee of $800. The license fee for the first year is the amount set in section 38a-11. The Division states that captive annual licensing fee as $375. Secretary of the State organization fees are not set in the captive sections.
Annual Maintenance Fee
The license authorizes business until 1 April and may be renewed (section 38a-91bb(e)). The Division states the captive annual licensing fee as $375. The formation page states that the license renewal and fee are due on or before 1 March. A dormant captive pays the same captive license renewal fee (section 38a-91uu(c)(3)).
Annual Filing Requirements
Section 38a-91gg requires a report of financial condition verified by the oath of two executive officers. The report is due before 1 March, and before 15 March for a pure captive or an industrial insured captive. Association captives and risk retention groups file in the form required by sections 38a-53 and 38a-53a. Reporting uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the commissioner requires, approves, or accepts statutory accounting principles or another comprehensive basis. A special purpose financial captive reports on statutory accounting principles unless the commissioner accepts another basis. A pure or industrial insured captive may be allowed to report on its fiscal year, with the report due not later than 75 days after fiscal year-end, and it still files calendar-year support before 15 March. A branch captive files copies of the reports required in the jurisdiction where the alien or foreign captive is formed, verified by two executive officers, on the domiciliary due date. The commissioner may waive the Connecticut annual report if those reports are adequate. A branch may be allowed to file 60 days after fiscal year-end. Bulletin FS-4C-25, dated 26 December 2025, states these due dates and adds an agency-captive due date of 1 March, with a permitted fiscal-year alternative of 75 days, and a dormant-captive due date of 15 March, with a permitted fiscal-year alternative of 75 days. The bulletin requires a statement of opinion on loss and loss-adjustment-expense reserves by a member of the American Academy of Actuaries or a qualified loss-reserve specialist. Under sections 38a-54 and 38a-91oo, each captive except a dormant captive files a financial report certified by an independent public accountant. The bulletin states that the audited report is due annually five months after the end of the fiscal accounting period. Premium tax on Form 207C is due 1 March. Filings are submitted to CID.CaptiveFilings@ct.gov. Applications for deviations and requests for waivers go to CID.CaptiveRequests@ct.gov. The board of directors, the committee of managers, or a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee meets at least once each year in Connecticut. The principal place of business is in Connecticut, and a registered agent is appointed. The formation page also states that one board member and a licensed agent must be Connecticut residents and that accounts are established with a bank in Connecticut. Section 38a-91hh requires an examination whenever the commissioner determines it to be prudent, and not less frequently than once every five years. The commissioner may waive that examination for a pure captive or for a branch captive of a pure captive. The examination is at the company's cost, with the salary limitation stated in the section. The formation page still describes an examination every three years that may be extended to five. That page predates the Public Act 22-118 revision of section 38a-91hh, and the current statute is followed here. A dormant captive files the 15 March financial-condition report and pays the renewal fee. The certificate of dormancy is renewed once every five years and is forfeited if insurance business resumes or the certificate is not timely renewed.
