British Virgin Islands

The British Virgin Islands Financial Services Commission licenses insurers under the Insurance Act, 2008, the Insurance Regulations, 2009 and the Regulatory Code, 2009. The Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2015 added Category E, a pure captive that may underwrite related-party business only, and Category F, a captive that underwrites related-party business and a maximum of unrelated business in order to qualify as an insurer under the law of a foreign jurisdiction. A Category C, D, E or F insurer must appoint a licensed insurance manager. A segregated portfolio company that wants an insurance licence is licensed as Category C. Minimum contributed capital under section 130 of the Code is $200,000 where the insurer may carry on any class of long-term business, and $100,000 otherwise. The Financial Services (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations, 2023, in force for these items from 1 April 2023, charge US$1,000 to apply for an insurer's licence, US$2,000 to issue a Category C, D, E or F licence, and US$7,500 to issue a Category C segregated-portfolio-company licence. Annual renewal for Category C, D, E or F runs from US$2,000 to US$7,500 by the preceding year's gross written premium, and a Category C segregated portfolio company pays US$7,500. The 2025 fee amendment did not change those insurance items. The Commission's 2024 annual report chart shows 44 captive insurers at the end of 2024, compared with 45 at the end of 2023, within 103 active insurance entities. Captive gross direct written premium was $75,404,920 and captive total assets were $1,510,902,604. The report does not publish formations, surrenders, a pure-versus-group split, or a staff count. The Insurance Act and the fee regulations do not impose a premium tax, and they do not state a corporate income-tax rate.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Contributed capital, general business Section 130 of the Regulatory Code sets the minimum contributed capital of a BVI insurer at $100,000 where the insurer is not authorised to carry on any class of long-term business. Section 10 of the Insurance Act requires the insurer to maintain at least the greater of that Code minimum and any higher amount the Commission directs.
Contributed capital, long-term business Section 130 of the Code sets the minimum at $200,000 where the insurer is authorised to carry on any class of long-term business. The same section applies to a BVI insurer that writes both long-term business and property and casualty business, because the long-term limb is triggered by authorisation for any class of long-term business.
Solvency margin, general insurer Section 131 of the Code requires a general insurer to maintain a minimum solvency margin of $100,000 if annual net written premium is less than $500,000; 20 percent of net annual written premium if that premium is greater than $500,000 but less than $5,000,000; and $1,000,000 plus 10 percent of the amount by which annual net written premium exceeds $5,000,000 if that premium is greater than $5,000,000. The section does not state the margin at exactly $500,000 or exactly $5,000,000. In the first financial year the premium measure is the amount projected in the business plan.
Solvency margin, long-term insurer Section 131 of the Code requires a long-term insurer to maintain a minimum solvency margin of $250,000. The insurer must in any event maintain a solvency margin adequate to meet its liabilities, taking into account the nature, size, complexity, structure and diversity of the business and its risk profile.
Commission direction The Commission may direct a higher contributed capital or a higher amount of capital resources, with at least 14 days to comply (Insurance Act, section 10). Section 129 of the Code requires capital resources adequate for the business in addition to the specific contributed-capital and solvency floors.

Regulatory Framework

Insurance Act, 2008 (Revised 2020 text published by the Commission), Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2015, Insurance Regulations, 2009, and Regulatory Code, 2009

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Insurance Act, 2008, as amended by the Insurance (Amendment) Act, 2015 (Act No. 16 of 2015), which inserted Category E and Category F. Licence categories are section 7. Contributed capital is section 10, and the dollar floors are Regulatory Code section 130. The solvency margin is section 12 of the Act and section 131 of the Code. An insurance manager for Category C, D, E or F is section 23. Directors are section 25. Segregated portfolio companies are section 36A. Audited financial statements are sections 56 and 57. Fees are the Financial Services (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations, 2023 (S.I. 2023 No. 5), which substituted the schedule from 1 April 2023. The annual due date remains regulation 2A of the Financial Services (Fees) Regulations, as substituted by S.I. 12 of 2017 and left in place by the 2023 substitution of the schedule. The Financial Services (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 (S.I. 2025 No. 4), in force on 2 January 2025, amended anti-money-laundering reporting-officer fees and did not amend the insurance items. The Insurance Guidelines, 2016 explain Categories E and F and segregated portfolio licensing. The captive annual return is filed under the Financial Services (Prudential and Statistical Returns) Order, 2009, as described in the Commission's captive-return guidance.

Licensing Speed
The Insurance Guidelines, 2016 state that, on receipt of a completed application, the Commission will endeavour to process the application and communicate its decision within the time stipulated in the Commission's Performance Accountability and Supervisory Service Standards. The guidelines do not state that number of days. The Insurance Act does not state a licensing-day standard.
Premium Tax Rate
No premium tax is imposed by the Insurance Act or the fee schedule. The regulatory charge is the fixed or premium-banded licence fee. No corporate income-tax rate was stated in the Commission legislation and reports reviewed.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Under the schedule substituted by the Financial Services (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations, 2023, with effect from 1 April 2023, the application fee for a Category A, B, C, D, E or F insurer's licence is US$1,000. The fee for issuing the licence is US$3,500 for Category A or B, US$2,000 for Category C, D, E or F, and US$7,500 for a Category C segregated portfolio company. An application to vary a licence by adding or removing a class of business, or by converting a category, is US$1,000, and approval of a variation is US$1,500 or US$2,000 for a conversion. An insurance manager's application fee is US$500 and the fee for issuing that licence is US$2,000. Registry incorporation fees are not set in the insurance rows of the schedule. The Commission's website FAQ still shows a single captive annual figure and is not the fee instrument used here.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual fees under the Insurance Act are due no later than 28 February (regulation 2A of the Financial Services (Fees) Regulations). The 2023 schedule sets annual renewal for a Category C, D, E or F licensee, by total gross written premiums of the preceding year, at US$2,000 where premiums are $500,000 or less, US$3,000 from $500,001 to $999,999, US$3,500 from $1,000,000 to $2,400,000, US$4,500 from $2,400,001 to $4,999,999, US$6,000 from $5,000,000 to $9,999,999, and US$7,500 at $10,000,000 and above. A Category C segregated portfolio company pays US$7,500. An insurance manager's annual renewal is US$2,000. The Financial Services (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025 did not change these insurance amounts.
Annual Filing Requirements
A BVI insurer submits financial statements within 6 months of the financial year end, or within a shorter prescribed period, together with an auditor's report and any report made to members (Insurance Act, section 57). The statements are approved by the directors and signed by at least one director (section 56). The insurer appoints an auditor approved by the Commission (section 61). The Commission may extend the section 57 period by up to 3 months in aggregate (section 59). A licensee in Category C, D, E or F also files the Captive Insurance Business Annual Return for the previous calendar year on or before 31 March, under the Commission's guidance on the Financial Services (Prudential and Statistical Returns) Order, 2009. The return goes to insurancereturns@bvifsc.vg. An extension of up to 30 days may be requested at least seven days before the deadline. Monetary amounts in the return are reported in US dollars. A Category C, D, E or F insurer appoints and at all times has an insurance manager that holds an insurance manager's licence, with the Commission's prior written approval (section 23). A BVI insurer has at least two directors, each an individual (section 25). A segregated portfolio company is licensed under section 36A. The Act does not state an in-Territory board-meeting requirement in the sections reviewed. The 20 March 2026 public statement directs general contact to commissioner@bvifsc.vg. The Commission's division email list also publishes Insurance@bvifsc.vg for the Insurance Division.
