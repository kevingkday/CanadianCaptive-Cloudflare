Federated States of Micronesia

The Federated States of Micronesia Insurance Board and the Insurance Commissioner license captive insurance companies under Chapter 10 of Title 37 of the FSM Code, the captive provisions added to the Insurance Act of 2006. Public Law No. 18-20, approved on 22 October 2013, sets four classes. A class 1 company insures its parent and affiliated companies or associations. A class 2 company may also insure related third-party businesses. A class 3 company is a member of a multiple corporate captive group. A class 4 company may also reinsure unaffiliated business. Section 1001(11), as amended by Public Law No. 18-20, describes that business as a company outside the parent's corporate system that has a business relationship with the parent or an affiliate, with commercial risks outside the Federated States of Micronesia, and says the definition does not include homeowners, personal automobile, or personal lines of FSM citizens. The same paragraph says the commercial risk is only reinsured by a class 4 company and is a maximum of 30 percent of net written premium after deducting ceded premium. Public Law No. 21-49, approved on 19 October 2019, requires minimum capital and surplus of $100,000 to be licensed. A multiple-corporate-captive core maintains $1,000,000 and each non-core member $100,000. A class 4 company maintains $5,000,000. The Commissioner may require more. The regulations published by the Insurance Board also set a class 2 test at the greater of $100,000, 20 percent of net premium income, or an amount the Commissioner determines, and a solvency margin at the greater of 20 percent of net premium income and 5 percent of loss reserves. Public Law No. 21-65, approved on 23 December 2019, places licensing with the Commissioner. Its purpose section says an application is deemed approved if it is neither approved nor denied within 30 days of receipt. The enacted section 1003A says the application is deemed approved if it is neither approved nor denied with 30 days of receipt. The nonrefundable application fee is $500 and the annual licence fee is $500. Section 1015 imposes a premium tax of 0.05 percent, due by 1 June, with an annual maximum of $20,000. Title 54, Chapter 3, as published by the Department of Finance and Administration, taxes a licensed captive as a major corporation at 21 percent of taxable income. The Insurance Board's 2025 annual report counts 23 licensed and active captives, out of 31 licensed since 2009, and says captives received $256 million of premium in 2025. The same report says two new captives were formed and licensed, says the Board approved one captive in 2025, and lists one licence dated 17 June 2025, so a single new-licence figure is not stored. The office is three board members, the Insurance Commissioner, and five employees. The report publishes info@fsminsuranceboard.com.

Tax Environment
Section 1015 requires each captive, on or before 1 June, to pay into the General Fund a tax of 0.05 percent of gross premiums for insurance on risks or property in the Federated States of Micronesia, and on risks and property elsewhere on which no premium tax is otherwise paid, for the year ending the preceding 31 March, less return premiums and less any reinsurance accepted. The annual maximum aggregate premium tax is $20,000. Two or more captives under common ownership and control are taxed as one. Common ownership and control is direct or indirect ownership of 80 percent or more of the voting stock, or of the surplus and voting power of mutual corporations. Multiyear premiums are prorated. Ten percent of the premium-tax revenue, or $150,000, whichever is greater, is transferred annually to the Insurance Board. Title 54, section 321, as published by the Department of Finance and Administration, imposes tax at 21 percent on the taxable income of every major corporation for a taxable year ending on or after 31 March 2011. A captive insurance company licensed under Title 37 is a major corporation regardless of capitalization. Taxable income is income before income taxes, determined under IFRS or GAAP as regularly used to calculate taxable income in the principal shareholder's place of incorporation or, if the shareholder is an individual, country of primary residence. A major corporation that is not engaging in business in the Federated States of Micronesia and that remits the section 321 tax is exempt from the gross-revenue tax for that taxable year. Foreign income tax is a non-refundable credit, capped at the FSM tax, with a carryforward of not more than seven years. The Insurance Board's captive-law page states the same 21 percent rate as a tax on net income and points to section 321 and to the section 1015 premium tax. The 2025 annual report says 2024 captive taxes were $17,126,164 and describes taxable income as underwriting revenue, interest on loans, and realized investment gains. That aggregate is not the section 1015 premium-tax cap.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Class 1 and class 2 Section 1007, as amended by Public Law No. 21-49, requires a captive insurance company to maintain minimum capital and surplus of $100,000 before a licence is issued. The 2014 code's additional requirement of $1,000,000 initial paid-in capital was removed by that public law. Regulation IX in the Insurance Board compilation sets class 1 capital at $100,000 and class 2 capital at the greater of $100,000, 20 percent of net premium income, or an amount determined by the Commissioner.
Class 3 multiple corporate captive The core member maintains minimum capital and surplus of $1,000,000. Each non-core member maintains $100,000. Section 1001 also requires the core to hold at least 5 percent of each other member and requires each member to seat at least one core director. The core is licensed before the other members. Regulation IX states the same dollar floors and allows the Commissioner to set a higher core amount.
Class 4 Minimum required capital and surplus of $5,000,000 under section 1007(3), as amended by Public Law No. 21-49. Regulation IX states the greater of $5,000,000 or an amount determined by the Commissioner. Section 1002(4), as amended by Public Law No. 18-20, says a class 4 company insures the risks of its parent and affiliated companies and/or associations and/or related third-party business and/or unaffiliated business and/or multiple corporate captive business. Section 1001(11) says unaffiliated commercial risk is only reinsured by a class 4 company, is outside the Federated States of Micronesia, does not include homeowners, personal automobile, or personal lines of FSM citizens, and is a maximum of 30 percent of net written premium after deducting ceded premium.
Commissioner increase and form The Commissioner may increase the minimum after taking into account the type, volume, and nature of the business and the potential risk retained. Capital and surplus may be any combination of cash, a letter of credit, investments permitted by section 1013, or any other security the Commissioner deems appropriate. A loan of the minimum capital and surplus required by section 1007 is prohibited.
Solvency margin Regulation X sets the minimum solvency margin at the greater of 20 percent of net premium income and 5 percent of the value of loss reserves. If net premium income is greater than $100 million, the captive may request that the Commissioner determine the margin. The 2014 code text does not restate this margin.
Dormant captive Section 1021, as renumbered by Public Law No. 22-02, allows a captive to apply for dormant status. The Commissioner may waive sections 1007, 1008, and 1013 if the captive certifies that it has no outstanding insurance liabilities on expired or terminated policies. The section does not state a substitute dollar floor.

Regulatory Framework

Title 37 of the Code of the Federated States of Micronesia, Chapter 10, Captive Insurance Act of 2006, as amended, and Title 54, Chapter 3, Corporate Income Tax Act of 2004, as amended

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Chapter 10 was added to the Insurance Act of 2006. The 2014 code text published by the FSM Legal Information System states the classes, the six-month report, the premium tax, and the examination cycle as then in force. Public Law No. 18-20, approved on 22 October 2013, added the class 4 definition and the unaffiliated-business limit. Public Law No. 21-49, approved on 19 October 2019, replaced section 1007 and removed the separate $1,000,000 initial paid-in capital figure that the 2014 code still shows. Public Law No. 21-65, approved on 23 December 2019, amended section 1003, added the 30-day deeming rules in sections 1003A, 1008A, 1013A, and 1016A, and restated dividends and investments. Public Law No. 21-199, approved on 5 November 2020, added dormancy, and Public Law No. 22-02, approved on 27 May 2021, renumbered that section as section 1021. Captive regulations are published by the Insurance Board in its captive laws and regulations compilation. Corporate income tax is Title 54, Chapter 3, including section 321, in the compilation published by the Department of Finance and Administration. Public Law No. 16-19, approved on 27 December 2009, made captive insurance companies subject to that chapter regardless of capitalization.

Licensing Speed
The purpose section of Public Law No. 21-65 says an application is deemed approved if it is neither approved nor denied within 30 days of receipt. The enacted section 1003A says the application shall be deemed approved if it is neither approved nor denied with 30 days of receipt by the Insurance Commissioner. Sections 1008A, 1013A, and 1016A use "within 30 days" for a dividend, a loan or investment, and a business-plan change.
Premium Tax Rate
0.05 percent of the gross premiums described in section 1015, due 1 June, with an annual maximum of $20,000. Captives under 80 percent common ownership are taxed as one captive. Separately, Title 54, section 321, taxes taxable income of a licensed captive at 21 percent. A major corporation that remits that tax and is not engaging in business in the Federated States of Micronesia is exempt from the gross-revenue tax for that year.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 1003, as amended by Public Law No. 21-65, charges a nonrefundable fee of $500 for examining, investigating, and processing the licence application. The Commissioner may retain legal, financial, and examination services and charge the reasonable cost to the applicant. A captive that receives a licence pays a further licence fee of $500 for the year of registration, due within 30 days after registration or licensure. Fees are deposited into the revolving fund. National registrar incorporation fees are not set in section 1003.
Annual Maintenance Fee
A yearly fee of $500 is due on or before the registration or licensure day and month each year. The 2025 annual report says the revolving fund receives application fees, licence fees, annual fees, and premium-tax amounts, and that charges for outside legal, financial, and examination services are also deposited there. At each fiscal year-end the fund is capped at $50,000 and the excess is transferred to the General Fund. Examination expenses are paid by the captive within 30 days of the warrant.
Annual Filing Requirements
Section 1010 of the 2014 code requires, within six months of the fiscal year-end, a report of financial condition verified by the oath of two executive officers, audited financial statements prepared under generally accepted accounting principles or international accounting standards unless the Commissioner approves a modification, and an opinion of a loss-reserve specialist. The auditor and the loss-reserve specialist must be approved. For a multiple corporate captive, the core files for itself and the members do not file separately. Regulation VI in the Insurance Board compilation requires the audited annual report within 90 calendar days after the fiscal year-end. The Code states six months and the published regulation states 90 days. Both are recorded. Title 54, section 351, requires the major-corporation income-tax return, with the financial statements, by the last day of the sixth month after the taxable year. Section 352 requires the tax return by the last business day of that sixth month and a deposit of 50 percent of the prior year's tax by the end of the eighth month of the current fiscal year. A newly formed captive has no prior year and is not required to pay that deposit. Premium tax is due on or before 1 June. Section 1003 requires a principal place of business in the Federated States of Micronesia, a principal representative approved by the Commissioner, and an annual board meeting in the Federated States of Micronesia. Only the principal representative must be physically present for the quorum. The other directors may attend by telephone. English insurance and accounting records are kept at the principal office for examination. Section 1011 requires an examination at least once in three years, which the Commissioner may extend to five years on petition, and whenever the Commissioner determines it to be prudent. The 2025 annual report says no captive examinations were conducted in fiscal year 2025. A dormant captive's section 1010 reports apply only to the first reports due after it becomes dormant, and the Commissioner may defer the examination, while the dormant captive continues. A captive may not insure the risks of individual citizens of the Federated States of Micronesia. Dividends out of capital or surplus require the Commissioner's prior approval, and a dividend proposal that is neither approved nor denied within 30 days of receipt is deemed approved.
