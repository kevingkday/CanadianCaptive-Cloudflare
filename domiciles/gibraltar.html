Gibraltar

Gibraltar authorises captive insurers and captive reinsurers under the Financial Services (Insurance Companies) Regulations 2020, made under the Financial Services Act 2019. The consolidated regulations were last updated on 30 June 2026. A captive insurer is owned by a financial undertaking other than an insurer or reinsurer, or by a non-financial undertaking, and its purpose is to insure only the risks of the undertaking or undertakings to which it belongs, or of its group. A captive reinsurer is defined in the same way for reinsurance. Regulation 116 sets the absolute floor of the minimum capital requirement at £2,400,000 for non-life business, including a captive insurer, £3,500,000 where any of classes 10 to 15 is covered, £3,500,000 for life business, including a captive insurer, £3,500,000 for a reinsurer, and £1,200,000 for a captive reinsurer. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission insurance-company page states the same floors. The minimum capital requirement must also lie between 25 and 45 percent of the solvency capital requirement, which regulation 91 calibrates at a 99.5 percent value-at-risk over one year. A company may be a protected cell company only with the Commission's written consent, and only if it is or will be a Gibraltar insurer or an authorised collective investment scheme, under the Protected Cell Companies Act 2001. Corporation tax on a company's taxable income is 15 percent for an accounting period. It was 10 percent through 31 July 2021 and 12.5 percent from 1 August 2021 until 30 June 2024. The pages reviewed do not publish a captive census, a staff count for insurance supervision, or a number of days in which an application must be decided.

Tax Environment
Rule 6(1) of the Rates of Tax Rules 1989 charges corporation tax at 15 percent of a company's taxable income for an accounting period. A utility is charged 20 percent, and the Commissioner may charge 20 percent where a company has abused a dominant market position. Rule 6C keeps the rate at 10 percent through 31 July 2021 and at 12.5 percent from 1 August 2021 for an accounting period that straddled 1 August 2021. Rule 6D keeps the rate at 12.5 percent through 30 June 2024 and at 15 percent from 1 July 2024 for an accounting period that straddled 1 July 2024. The Rates of Tax Rules do not impose an insurance premium tax. No separate premium-tax instrument was retrieved.

Protected cell Regulation 116 states the absolute floor for the insurance or reinsurance undertaking. It does not state a separate absolute floor for a protected cell. Section 5 of the Protected Cell Companies Act 2001 requires the directors to keep cellular assets separate from non-cellular assets and to keep each cell's cellular assets separate from the other cells.
How the minimum is set Regulation 115 requires eligible basic own funds covering the minimum capital requirement. Regulation 116(1) calibrates the linear calculation to an 85 percent value-at-risk of basic own funds over one year and then sets absolute floors. Without displacing those floors, regulation 116(3) requires the minimum capital requirement to be at least 25 percent and no more than 45 percent of the solvency capital requirement, including any capital add-on. Regulation 90 requires eligible own funds covering the solvency capital requirement. Regulation 91(4) sets that requirement at a 99.5 percent value-at-risk of basic own funds over one year. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission insurance-company page states the same absolute floors and the same 99.5 percent solvency capital requirement.

Regulatory Framework

Financial Services Act 2019, and the Financial Services (Insurance Companies) Regulations 2020

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Financial Services Act 2019, dated 23 July 2019. Financial Services (Insurance Companies) Regulations 2020 (Legal Notice 2020/044), in operation from 15 January 2020, consolidated text last updated 30 June 2026. The captive insurer and captive reinsurer definitions are regulation 3(1). Permission and the scheme of operations are regulations 16 and 17. Own funds covering the solvency capital requirement are regulation 90. The 99.5 percent calibration is regulation 91(4). The minimum capital requirement and its absolute floors are regulations 115 and 116. Quarterly reporting of the minimum capital requirement is regulation 116(4). Protected Cell Companies Act 2001, including section 5 on cellular and non-cellular assets and section 11 on the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission's consent. Financial Services (Fees) Regulations 2020, consolidated through the amendments commencing on 1 April 2026 and 3 September 2026, Fee Block B1 and Schedules 1A and 1B. Financial Services (Insurance Supervisory Reporting) (Technical Standards) Regulations 2025, in operation from 31 January 2025, Article 2A, for the solvency and financial condition report deadline. Rates of Tax Rules 1989, made under the Income Tax Act 2010, rule 6, as updated to 1 July 2024, including the transitional rules 6C and 6D.

Licensing Speed
The insurance-company page describes a three-stage application and says the Commission moves the file to the next stage when it is satisfied with the current stage. It does not state a number of days for a decision. Applicants request access to the submission system by email to authorisations@fsc.gi.
Premium Tax Rate
No premium tax is imposed by the Rates of Tax Rules 1989. Corporation tax is 15 percent of taxable income from 1 July 2024, after 10 percent through 31 July 2021 and 12.5 percent from 1 August 2021 to 30 June 2024.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Schedule 2, Fee Block B1, of the Financial Services (Fees) Regulations 2020 charges £10,500 for an application for Part 7 permission by a captive insurer, whether or not it is structured as a protected cell company. Approval of an additional protected cell is £3,500 if the Commission assesses it as non-complex, £11,000 if moderately complex, and £40,000 if complex. Unless the Commission agrees otherwise, the application is accompanied by the £3,500 and the balance of a higher fee is paid before the application is regarded as complete. The application fee is paid when the application is made. The fee regulation defines a captive insurer, for fee purposes, as an insurance or reinsurance company wholly owned by a parent which covers only risks of the group and no third-party risks. That fee definition is narrower than regulation 3(1) of the Insurance Companies Regulations. Incorporation fees of the company registry are not set in Schedule 2.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Fee Block B1 charges a captive insurer, including one set up as a protected cell company, a base fee of £9,192 plus £2,625 for each cell, plus a gross-premium-income fee, a gross-technical-liabilities fee, a passporting fee, an internal-capital-model fee, and a matching-adjustment fee where those items apply. For a non-life captive the premium fee is £198 per £1,000,000 or part on the first £10,000,000 of gross premium income, then £164, £133, £100, and £65 per £1,000,000 or part on the higher bands. For a life captive the first band is £275 per £1,000,000 or part, then £253, £230, £206, and £185. The gross-technical-liabilities fee starts at £40 per £1,000,000 or part on the first £10,000,000 and then uses the lower rates in the schedule. Passporting is £1,030 on a services basis and £5,150 on an establishment basis. An approved internal capital model, and an approved matching adjustment, each add a monitoring fee of £51,500. A life captive insurer, including a protected cell company, also pays the Schedule 1B anti-money-laundering supervision fee of £4,017. Where the Commission undertakes group supervision, Schedule 1A adds £3,605, £11,330, or £41,200 according to the Commission's complexity assessment. The Schedule 1 fee is due within 28 days of the notice that permission has been granted and, after that, within 28 days from 1 April each year, unless the Commission agrees otherwise. A late fee draws an administrative fee of £250 and interest at 5 percent a year above base rate.
Annual Filing Requirements
Regulation 116(4) requires the minimum capital requirement to be calculated at least quarterly and the result reported to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission. Article 2A of the Financial Services (Insurance Supervisory Reporting) (Technical Standards) Regulations 2025 requires the solvency and financial condition report to be disclosed no later than 70 business days after the financial year-end, and no later than 100 business days where the group report under regulation 233 applies. The same article requires submission of the own-risk-and-solvency-assessment report and the annual, half-yearly, and quarterly quantitative templates. Regulation 37 allows the Commission to limit reporting that would otherwise be more frequent than annual, where the burden is excessive for the risks and the information is still reported at least annually. That limit is not available merely because the undertaking is a captive, and regulation 116(4) is preserved. The regulations reviewed do not state that a board meeting must be held in Gibraltar.
