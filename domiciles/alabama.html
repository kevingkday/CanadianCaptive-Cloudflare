Alabama

Alabama licenses captive insurers under the Alabama Captive Insurers Act, Code of Alabama sections 27-31B-1 to 27-31B-28, enacted as Act 2006-509. The Department of Insurance states that the Legislature passed the bill in the 2006 regular session and that Governor Bob Riley signed it. Act 2016-191, Act 2021-162, and Act 2026-486 have amended the chapter. The Department administers the Act. A captive may be a pure, agency, association, industrial insured, protected cell, incorporated cell, reinsurance, or special purpose financial captive, or a captive risk retention group, and it may be formed as a stock, mutual, nonprofit, limited liability, or reciprocal insurer. A branch captive is an alien captive licensed for Alabama operations and is a pure captive for those operations unless the commissioner permits otherwise. Unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus is at least $250,000 for a pure captive or a protected cell captive, $500,000 for an agency, association, or industrial insured captive, $1,000,000 for a reinsurance captive or a risk retention group, and $250,000 for a branch captive. The commissioner may set another actuarially supported amount for a pure, agency, association, protected cell, or risk retention group captive. A reciprocal maintains free surplus of $1,000,000. Section 27-31B-6 does not state a separate dollar floor for a special purpose financial captive or for an individual incorporated cell. Direct premium tax is 0.4 percent, 0.3 percent, 0.2 percent, and 0.075 percent on successive $20 million bands. Assumed-reinsurance tax is 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent. The minimum is $5,000 and the maximum is $100,000. No tax is due for a calendar year in which the captive writes no premiums. The minimum is prorated if the captive is licensed or surrenders its license for less than a full year. Rule 482-1-138-.20, effective 1 January 2022, sets the adjusted application fee at $240 and the initial and annual license fee at $360. The Department's fee schedule also lists a $200 application examination fee. The commissioner may issue a provisional license for not more than 60 days. A temporary moratorium on new captive and domestic risk-retention-group filings ended on 30 June 2026. The Department's 1 July 2026 bulletin says new filings are accepted and processed in the ordinary course. The Department does not publish a captive census or a staff count. The captive tax form names the Examination Division at ExaminationDivision@insurance.alabama.gov.

Tax Environment
Section 27-31B-16 requires the tax by 1 March, paid to the commissioner. Direct premiums, after return premiums and policyholder dividends or premium deposits returned or credited, are taxed at four-tenths of one percent on the first $20 million, three-tenths of one percent on the next $20 million, two-tenths of one percent on the next $20 million, and seventy-five thousandths of one percent thereafter. Annuity considerations are not taxed. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, 0.050 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.025 percent thereafter. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer. If the calculated tax is under $5,000, the tax is $5,000. The aggregate tax may not exceed $100,000. Because the $100,000 maximum is reached inside the second direct-premium band, the 0.2 percent and 0.075 percent direct bands do not increase the tax once that maximum applies. The Department's tax form, revised 6 September 2024, states the direct schedule as 0.4 percent up to $20 million and then $80,000 plus 0.3 percent of the excess, not to exceed $100,000. For a protected cell captive, the $5,000 minimum applies to the company as a whole and not to each protected cell. The maximum paid by a protected cell captive is the aggregate of the tax liabilities of each protected cell, and the maximum attributed to any one protected cell captive is $100,000. Examination expenses under section 27-31B-10 and business-privilege tax paid under section 40-14A-22 are credits for the calendar year in which they were paid. A credit that would reduce the tax below zero produces a tax of zero and is not carried forward. The premium tax is all of the tax collectible under Alabama law from a captive, except business-privilege tax and ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used to produce income. The chapter does not state the business-privilege-tax rate. Two or more captives under common ownership and control are taxed as one captive. For stock corporations, common ownership and control is direct or indirect ownership of 80 percent or more of the outstanding voting stock. For mutual corporations, it is direct or indirect ownership of 80 percent or more of the surplus and the voting power. Branch tax applies only to branch business. If the captive writes no premiums during the calendar year, the tax is not applicable. If a captive is licensed for less than a full year and the calculated tax is below the minimum, the minimum is 100 percent, 75 percent, 50 percent, or 25 percent according to whether the license date falls on or before 31 March, from 1 April to 30 June, from 1 July to 30 September, or from 1 October to 31 December. A surrender is prorated at 25 percent, 50 percent, 75 percent, or 100 percent on those same bands. The Department's tax form states the same rates, the $360 renewal fee, the proration, and the credits.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive Not less than $250,000, or another amount determined by the commissioner and actuarially supported by a feasibility study (section 27-31B-6(a)(1)).
Agency captive Not less than $500,000, or another amount determined by the commissioner and actuarially supported by a feasibility study (section 27-31B-6(a)(2)).
Association captive Not less than $500,000, or another amount determined by the commissioner and actuarially supported by a feasibility study (section 27-31B-6(a)(3)).
Industrial insured captive Not less than $500,000 (section 27-31B-6(a)(4)).
Reinsurance captive Not less than $1,000,000 (section 27-31B-6(a)(5)). A reinsurance captive only assumes risks from an insurer or reinsurer approved or licensed in Alabama or in a foreign or alien jurisdiction the commissioner approves, and it assumes those risks on a funds-withheld basis or another basis the commissioner accepts (section 27-31B-2(32)).
Protected cell captive Not less than $250,000, or another amount determined by the commissioner and actuarially supported by a feasibility study (section 27-31B-6(a)(6)). Section 27-31B-6 does not state a separate dollar floor for an individual protected cell or for an incorporated cell. The sponsors provide the minimum capital and surplus (section 27-31B-2(29)).
Branch captive Not less than $250,000 (section 27-31B-6(a)(7)). As security for liabilities attributable to the branch operations, the commissioner may require collateral equal to the net loss reserves on branch business plus other insurance liabilities the commissioner determines, maintained in a manner acceptable to the commissioner (section 27-31B-6(e)). A branch captive is a pure captive for its Alabama operations unless the commissioner permits otherwise (section 27-31B-2(7)).
Risk retention group Not less than $1,000,000, or another amount determined by the commissioner and actuarially supported by a feasibility study (section 27-31B-6(a)(8)).
Reciprocal captive Free surplus of $1,000,000, notwithstanding the amounts in subsection (a) (section 27-31B-6(b)).
Special purpose financial captive Section 27-31B-2(33) defines a special purpose financial captive as a captive that has received a certificate of authority for the limited purposes provided in the chapter. Section 27-31B-6 does not state a separate dollar floor for that type.
Additional capital and form The commissioner may prescribe additional capital and surplus based on the type, volume, and nature of the business (section 27-31B-6(c)). Capital and surplus may be cash, cash equivalents, a surplus note, securities meeting section 27-6-3, or, if the commissioner approves, a clean, irrevocable, and unconditional letter of credit issued by an Alabama-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank. No assets of the captive may be pledged or encumbered for payment of the letter of credit (section 27-31B-6(d)). A captive may be capitalized with a surplus note on terms the section permits.

Regulatory Framework

Alabama Captive Insurers Act, Code of Alabama Title 27, Chapter 31B, sections 27-31B-1 to 27-31B-28, and Alabama Administrative Code Chapter 482-1-138

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Chapter 31B originated in Act 2006-509. The code history of the licensing section cites Act 2008-391, Act 2016-191, Act 2021-162, and Act 2026-486. Capital and surplus are section 27-31B-6. The statutory fee amounts are section 27-31B-4. The adjusted fees in force are Rule 482-1-138-.20, effective 1 January 2022, adopted under sections 27-31B-4 and 41-1-11. Reports are section 27-31B-9, amended by Act 2016-191 and Act 2026-486. Examinations are section 27-31B-10. Premium tax is section 27-31B-16, amended by Act 2016-191. Protected cell captives are sections 27-31B-22 to 27-31B-25. Captive risk retention groups are also subject to section 27-31B-28, and Rule 482-1-138-.17.1 lists additional Title 27 chapters that apply to them. Chapter 482-1-138 was new on 1 August 2006 and effective 11 August 2006, and later rules in the chapter were revised through 2021.

Licensing Speed
Section 27-31B-3(g) allows the commissioner to issue a provisional license for not more than 60 days when the public interest will be served, after a complete application, payment of the licensure fees, and a preliminary finding that the persons who will control and manage the captive are acceptable. The provisional license ends automatically if the regular process is not completed within 60 days. The Department does not publish a standard number of days for an ordinary license. The 1 July 2026 bulletin states that new filings are accepted and processed in the ordinary course.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.4 percent, 0.3 percent, 0.2 percent, and 0.075 percent on successive $20 million bands. The Department's tax form states the direct calculation as 0.4 percent up to $20 million and $80,000 plus 0.3 percent of the excess, not to exceed $100,000. Assumed reinsurance: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $20 million bands. Annual minimum $5,000, prorated if the captive is licensed or surrenders for less than a full year and the calculated tax is below the minimum. Annual maximum $100,000. For a protected cell captive the minimum applies to the company as a whole, and the company maximum is $100,000. No tax if no premiums are written. Due 1 March. Examination expenses and business-privilege tax are credits and cannot reduce the tax below zero.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 27-31B-4 states a $200 fee for filing the license application and a $300 license fee for the initial license and each annual renewal. Rule 482-1-138-.20, effective 1 January 2022, adjusts those amounts under section 41-1-11 to a $240 application fee and a $360 initial and annual license fee. The Department's captive application fee schedule, citing Act 2021-162 effective 1 July 2021, lists a $240 application fee and a $200 application examination fee, both paid with the initial online application, and a $360 fee paid on receipt of the tentative-approval email. The commissioner may also charge the reasonable cost of outside legal, financial, and examination services under section 27-2-25. Secretary of State organization fees are not set in Chapter 31B.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual license renewal fee of $360 under Rule 482-1-138-.20 and the Department's 6 September 2024 captive tax form. Section 27-31B-3(f) states that a license authorizes business until 1 April and may be renewed. The renewal fee is reported on line 5 of the captive fee and tax return and is paid with the premium tax by 1 March.
Annual Filing Requirements
Before 1 March, each captive submits a report of its financial condition verified by the oath of two executive officers (section 27-31B-9). Reporting uses statutory accounting principles unless the commissioner approves generally accepted accounting principles, except as section 27-31B-6 provides. An association captive, and an industrial insured captive insuring an industrial insured group under section 27-31B-2(19)b, files in the form required by section 27-3-26. A risk retention group files in that form and complies with the casualty-insurer filing requirements. The Department's annual-statement page assigns the NAIC property-and-casualty blank to association captives and to industrial insured captives of a section 27-31B-2(19)b group, the pure or industrial-insured captive statement to pure captives and to industrial insured captives of a section 27-31B-2(19)a group, and the NAIC protected-cell blank to sponsored captives. A pure captive, or an industrial insured captive of a section 27-31B-2(19)a group, may be allowed to report on a fiscal year. The report is then due 60 days after fiscal year-end, and pages 1, 2, 3, and 5 of the pure or industrial-insured statement for the calendar year-end are still filed before 1 March. A branch captive files, 60 days after fiscal year-end, copies of the reports required where the alien captive is formed. The commissioner may waive the captive annual statement for business written in the alien jurisdiction if the domiciliary report is adequate. On or before 30 June, an audited statement of financial condition for the preceding calendar year is prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States and includes the independent certified public accountant's report, a balance sheet, an income statement, a statement of cash flows, a statement of changes in capital and surplus, and notes (section 27-31B-9(e)). An annual actuarial certification of loss and loss-expense reserves, by a qualified actuary as defined in the NAIC property-and-casualty statement instructions and approved by the commissioner, is filed with the 1 March report. Rule 482-1-138-.04 also requires the audit by 30 June and states that a captive other than a captive risk retention group, with direct premiums under $2,200,000 and fewer than 1,000 policyholders or certificate holders, or with assumed premiums of $2,200,000 or less, may apply for an annual exemption from the audit. The exemption is renewed annually. A captive risk retention group may apply for a hardship exemption from the audit, and that exemption does not remove the reserve certification. The board of directors or managers, or a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee, meets at least once each year in Alabama. The principal place of business is in Alabama, or the branch maintains a place of business for its branch operations. A registered agent is appointed. The captive owns an account at an Alabama bank or credit union with a physical location the commissioner approves, or at a veteran-related financial institution the commissioner approves. It employs or contracts with a captive manager, an independent certified public accountant, and an actuary, each meeting the commissioner's standards. The manager, or the manager's officers and directors, may not serve as president except where the captive contracts with its parent, an affiliate, or a controlled unaffiliated business for management. Section 27-31B-10 requires an examination at least once in three years and whenever the commissioner determines it to be prudent. On application, the commissioner may enlarge the period to five years if the captive has a comprehensive annual audit of a satisfactory scope by approved independent auditors. The examination is at the captive's cost under section 27-2-25. A branch examination is limited to branch business and branch operations if the branch annually provides a certificate of compliance from its home jurisdiction. The commissioner may issue a provisional license for not more than 60 days after a complete application, payment of the fees, and a preliminary finding that the persons who will control and manage the captive are acceptable. The chapter as published does not contain a dormant-captive status. Bulletins 2025-01 and 2025-05 imposed a temporary moratorium on new captive and domestic risk-retention-group filings. The Department's 1 July 2026 bulletin terminated that moratorium effective immediately and states that new filings are accepted and processed in the ordinary course. The captives page still links the earlier moratorium bulletins.
