Montana

Montana licenses captive insurers under Title 33, Chapter 28, MCA, enacted in 2001 and administered by the Captive Insurance Team of the Commissioner of Securities and Insurance. The chapter covers pure, association, industrial insured, special purpose, protected cell, branch, and captive reinsurance companies, and captive risk retention groups. Protected cells must be incorporated. A special purpose captive's capital is an amount the commissioner sets after reviewing the business plan, and a special purpose captive may use a series of members in a limited liability company. The 2025 CSI brochure states that the captive count is 279: 56 pure, 9 special purpose, 12 reinsurance, 1 association, 3 protected cell structures, 17 protected cells, 9 series LLC structures, 165 series business units, and 7 risk retention groups. The same brochure states that 41 captives were licensed in 2024, that 26 companies had been formed as of 1 June 2025, and that Montana has licensed more than 900 captives since 2001. The captive page states that Montana has nearly 300 domiciled captives, that captive insurance contributes $5 million annually to the Montana economy, and that Montana is the fifth-largest U.S. captive domicile. Unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus is at least $250,000 for a pure captive and $500,000 for an association captive, an industrial insured captive, or a captive risk retention group. A protected cell captive holds at least $500,000, which the commissioner may reduce to not less than $250,000 if the core assumes no risk, the cells' risks are homogeneous, and there are not more than 10 cells. A captive reinsurance company holds at least 50 percent of the capital required for its type. A dormant captive maintains at least $25,000. Direct premium tax is 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent thereafter. Assumed-reinsurance premium tax is 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.050 percent. The annual minimum is $5,000 and the annual maximum is $100,000. The maximum does not apply to a protected cell captive or to a special purpose captive with a series of members. The application fee is $200 and the license and annual renewal fee is $300, charged as well to each series and each protected cell. The certificate is effective until 1 March. Examinations of captives other than risk retention groups are at the commissioner's discretion. A captive risk retention group is examined at least every five years. No standard licensing turnaround is published. The published contact is the Captive Insurance Team at captives@mt.gov.

Tax Environment
Section 33-28-201 requires the tax by 1 March. Direct premiums, after return premiums and policyholder dividends or premium deposits returned or credited, are taxed at 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent on each subsequent dollar. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.050 percent thereafter. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer. If the direct and assumed taxes are under $5,000, the tax is $5,000. In the year a captive subject to the minimum is first authorized, or surrenders its certificate, the minimum is prorated by quarter. Each protected cell is considered separately in determining the aggregate tax. Each series of members of a special purpose captive is considered separately, except that the minimum tax is considered in the aggregate. The annual maximum is $100,000. That maximum does not apply to a protected cell captive or to a special purpose captive with a series of members. Two or more captives under common ownership and control, defined as 80 percent of the voting stock or, for mutuals, 80 percent of the surplus and voting power, are taxed as one captive. Only the branch business of a branch captive is taxed. A dormant captive is not liable for the section 33-28-201 tax. It pays a $1,000 annual dormancy tax by 1 March for any portion of the preceding year in which it held the certificate, and each series or protected cell is considered separate for that $1,000 tax. The certificate of dormancy expires after five consecutive years and may not be renewed. The captive page requires electronic premium-tax filing.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive Not less than $250,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus (33-28-104(1)(a)).
Association captive Not less than $500,000 (33-28-104(1)(c)).
Industrial insured captive, including a captive risk retention group Not less than $500,000 (33-28-104(1)(b)).
Special purpose captive An amount determined by the commissioner after considering the business plan, feasibility study, and pro forma documents, including the nature of the risks to be insured (33-28-104(1)(d)). The statute does not set a dollar floor.
Protected cell captive Not less than $500,000. If the protected cell captive assumes no risks, the risks insured by the cells are homogeneous, and there are not more than 10 cells, the commissioner may reduce the amount to not less than $250,000 (33-28-104(1)(e)). Section 33-28-101 requires every protected cell to be incorporated. The statute does not set a separate dollar floor for an individual cell.
Branch captive Not less than the capital and surplus required for the foreign captive's organizational form under 33-28-104(1)(a) through (1)(e). The minimum is held jointly by the commissioner and the branch in a Federal Reserve System bank the commissioner approves.
Captive reinsurance company Not less than 50 percent of the capital that would be required for that type of captive (33-28-104(1)(g)).
Dormant captive Not less than $25,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus while a certificate of dormancy is in force (33-28-401). A risk retention group cannot be dormant under that section.
Form of capital Cash, a cash equivalent, or an irrevocable letter of credit on the commissioner's form, issued by a Montana-chartered bank, a Federal Reserve member bank, or another state-chartered bank the commissioner approves. The commissioner may require additional capital based on the type, volume, and nature of the business (33-28-104).

Regulatory Framework

Montana Captive Insurance Companies Act, Title 33, Chapter 28, Montana Code Annotated, and ARM Title 6, Chapter 6, Subchapter 68

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Title 33, Chapter 28, MCA, enacted by Chapter 298, Laws of 2001, and published as the 2025 Montana Code Annotated. Capital and surplus are 33-28-104. The certificate, lines of business, in-state meeting, and fees are 33-28-102. Reports are 33-28-107. Examinations are 33-28-108. Premium tax is 33-28-201, amended by Chapter 548, Laws of 2025. Dormant captives are 33-28-401. The Commissioner cites ARM 6.6.6801 through 6.6.6821 for the annual audit and ARM 6.6.6816 for certification of loss reserves.

Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent thereafter. Assumed reinsurance: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.050 percent. Annual minimum $5,000, prorated by quarter in the year of first authorization and in the year the certificate is surrendered. Annual maximum $100,000, which does not apply to a protected cell captive or to a special purpose captive with a series of members. Due 1 March. A dormant captive pays a $1,000 dormancy tax instead of the premium tax.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 33-28-102(4) requires a nonrefundable fee of $200 for examining, investigating, and processing the application of each captive, each individual series of members, and each protected cell. The commissioner may retain outside legal, financial, and examination services and charge the reasonable cost to the applicant. The captive page states the same $200 application fee together with a $300 license fee. Secretary of State organization fees are not set in Chapter 28.
Annual Maintenance Fee
A fee of $300 for the year of registration and a renewal fee of $300 for each later year, payable by each captive, each individual series of members, and each protected cell (33-28-102(4)(b)). The certificate is effective until 1 March and may be renewed. A dormant captive is not otherwise liable for the section 33-28-102 annual renewal and instead pays the $1,000 dormancy tax.
Annual Filing Requirements
On or before 1 April, a captive other than a risk retention group submits a report of its financial condition, verified by two executive officers. A captive risk retention group submits its report on or before 1 March and files the NAIC property-and-casualty blank with the commissioner and with the NAIC, on an annual and quarterly basis. Reporting uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the commissioner requires statutory accounting principles. A captive other than a risk retention group may be allowed to report on a fiscal year-end. The report is then due 90 days after fiscal year-end, and a report supporting the premium-tax return is still filed before 1 April for the prior calendar year. A branch captive files, by 1 April, copies of the reports required in the jurisdiction where the foreign captive is formed. Premium tax is due 1 March. The captive page states that premium tax is filed electronically, and it states that captives other than risk retention groups may file the Montana short-form annual statement by 1 April. The same page states that ARM 6.6.6801 through 6.6.6821 require an annual audit by an independent certified public accountant the commissioner approves, and that ARM 6.6.6816 requires loss and loss-expense reserves to be certified by a Fellow of the Casualty Actuarial Society, a member of the American Academy of Actuaries, or another person who has demonstrated competence in loss-reserve evaluation and whom the commissioner approves. The board, the board of managing members, or a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee meets at least once each year in Montana. The principal place of business is in Montana, and at least one director, or one manager of a limited liability company, is a Montana resident. A captive risk retention group has at least five directors. Books and records needed for a statutory examination are kept in Montana. The commissioner may examine a captive as often as the commissioner considers advisable. A captive risk retention group is examined at least every five years. The examination is at the captive's cost. A dormant captive files a financial-condition report within 90 days of each fiscal year-end and is not subject to examination under 33-28-108 while the certificate of dormancy is in force.
