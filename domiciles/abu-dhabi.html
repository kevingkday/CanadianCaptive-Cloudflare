Abu Dhabi

Abu Dhabi Global Market licenses captive insurers under the Captive Insurance Business Rulebook of the Financial Services Regulatory Authority. The current text is CIB VER07.270426, effective 27 April 2026. The first version, CIB VER01.041015, took effect on 21 October 2015. ADGM was established as a financial free zone under Federal Decree No. 15 of 2013. Rule 1.3.3 states that an insurer that is not incorporated in ADGM cannot be a captive insurer. The Rules authorise four classes. A Class 1 captive insures only risks of the group to which it belongs. A Class 2 captive may obtain no more than 20 percent of gross written premium from third-party risks arising from business or operations closely linked to that group. A Class 3 captive insures only persons engaged in similar, related, or common business, activities, trade, services, or operations, and it is owned by those persons or by a body corporate of which all of them are members. A Class 4 captive is an entity that the Regulator decides does not meet Class 1, 2, or 3. A cell company may carry on the business through class captive cells. Minimum capital is the highest of a base amount, a premium-risk component, and a technical-provision component, and the Regulator may direct a higher amount. The base is $150,000 for Class 1, $250,000 for Class 2, $500,000 for Class 3, and $1 million for Class 4. The Regulator may set a different base for Class 2, Class 3, and Class 4. The premium-risk component is 18 percent of net written premium up to $5 million and 16 percent above that. The technical-provision component is 5 percent of the net claims reserve for general business and 2.5 percent of actuarial policyholder liabilities for long-term business. A cell company holds at least $50,000 of non-cellular eligible capital. An active cell maintains the higher of net cellular assets of at least $50,000 and a risk-based test using the same premium and technical-provision percentages. The Fees Rulebook in force from 1 January 2026, FEES VER20.010126, charges $5,000 to apply and $5,000 a year to supervise a captive insurer. A cell company pays an additional $8,000, plus $1,000 for each cell, on application and each year, and $1,000 to add a cell later. The Rulebook does not impose a premium tax. UAE corporate tax is Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022. A Qualifying Free Zone Person pays 0 percent on Qualifying Income and 9 percent on taxable income that is not Qualifying Income. Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025 lists reinsurance services as a Qualifying Activity and lists insurance activities regulated under Federal Decree-Law No. 48 of 2023 as an Excluded Activity, without prejudice to reinsurance services and headquarter services to Related Parties. The FSRA does not publish a captive census, a staff count, or a licensing-day standard. Authorisation enquiries go to authorisation@adgm.com. A captive may outsource managerial functions to a captive insurance manager and not to any other party.

Tax Environment
The Captive Insurance Business Rulebook does not impose a premium tax or an annual premium-tax return. UAE corporate tax is imposed by Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022. Article 3 imposes 0 percent on the portion of taxable income that does not exceed the amount set by Cabinet decision and 9 percent on taxable income above that amount. A Qualifying Free Zone Person pays 0 percent on Qualifying Income and 9 percent on taxable income that is not Qualifying Income. The Federal Tax Authority's free-zone bulletin states that a Qualifying Free Zone Person is not eligible for the 0 percent rate on the first AED 375,000 and pays 9 percent on its entire taxable income that is not Qualifying Income. Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025 lists reinsurance services as a Qualifying Activity. It lists insurance activities, defined as insurance operations regulated under Federal Decree-Law No. 48 of 2023, as an Excluded Activity, without prejudice to reinsurance services and to headquarter services to Related Parties. Income from an Excluded Activity is not eligible for the 0 percent rate. Whether a particular ADGM captive is a Qualifying Free Zone Person, and whether a particular contract is reinsurance, headquarter services, or an excluded insurance activity, is determined under those federal instruments and is not set by a captive class in the CIB. The 2017 ADGM captive brochure states a 0 percent tax environment until 2065. That date is not restated in CIB VER07.270426, in FEES VER20.010126, or in Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022. No captive-specific value-added-tax rate is stated in the Rulebook.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
How the minimum is set The minimum capital requirement is the highest of the base capital requirement, the premium-risk component, and the technical-provision risk component (CIB Rule 2.2.1). The Regulator may direct a higher minimum. A captive must also hold additional financial resources adequate for the nature, size, and complexity of its business (Rule 2.1.2).
Class 1 captive insurer Base capital of $150,000 (Rule 2.2.2(a)). A Class 1 captive may effect or carry out contracts of insurance only for risks related to or arising out of the business or operations of the group to which it belongs (Rule 1.4).
Class 2 captive insurer Base capital of $250,000, unless the Regulator sets a different amount (Rule 2.2.2(b)). Third-party gross written premium is limited to 20 percent and must arise from business or operations closely linked to the group (Rule 1.5).
Class 3 captive insurer Base capital of $500,000, unless the Regulator sets a different amount (Rule 2.2.2(c)). Cover is limited to persons in a similar, related, or common business, activity, trade, service, or operation, and the captive is owned by those persons or by a body corporate of which all of them are members (Rule 1.6).
Class 4 captive insurer Base capital of $1 million, unless the Regulator sets a different amount (Rule 2.2.2(d)). Class 4 is an entity that the Regulator decides does not meet Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 (Rule 1.7).
Premium-risk component 18 percent of net written premium up to $5 million, plus 16 percent of net written premium above $5 million, for every class (Rule 2.2.3). The same formula applies to a captive cell (Rule 4.6).
Technical-provision component For general insurance business, 5 percent of the net claims reserve, including incurred-but-not-reported claims and claims-settlement expenses, net of expected reinsurance and other recoveries (Rule 2.2.4). For long-term insurance business, 2.5 percent of policyholder liabilities calculated using actuarial methods (Rule 2.2.5). A captive cell uses the same 5 percent and 2.5 percent components (Rules 4.7 and 4.8).
Cell company and each active cell A captive insurer that is a cell company must at all times hold non-cellular eligible capital of at least $50,000 (Rule 4.2.1). An active cell must maintain the higher of net cellular assets of at least $50,000 and the cell's risk-based minimum solvency requirement (Rules 4.4 and 4.5). Captive insurance business of a cell company is conducted only through captive cells (Rule 4.3).
Form of capital Chapter 3 sets eligible capital. A qualifying letter of credit may be included where the Regulator allows it. Rule 3.1.4 requires the letter of credit to be unconditional and irrevocable, for a fixed amount, renewable annually, and issued by a bank rated at least BBB+ by Standard & Poor's, or the equivalent by another approved rating agency, and regulated in ADGM or another jurisdiction the Regulator approves. The captive must not be required to give collateral to the bank for the letter of credit.

Qualifying Activities and Excluded Activities for a Qualifying Free Zone Person are Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025.

Premium Tax Rate
No premium tax is imposed by the Captive Insurance Business Rulebook. UAE corporate tax under Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 is 9 percent above the Cabinet threshold, and 0 percent on Qualifying Income of a Qualifying Free Zone Person. Non-qualifying income of a Qualifying Free Zone Person is taxed at 9 percent, without the AED 375,000 0 percent band. Reinsurance services are a Qualifying Activity. Insurance activities regulated under Federal Decree-Law No. 48 of 2023 are an Excluded Activity, without prejudice to reinsurance services and headquarter services to Related Parties.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
FEES Rule 3.11.3 requires an application fee of $5,000 for a financial-services permission to effect or carry out contracts of insurance as a captive insurer or as an insurance special purpose vehicle. An applicant that will operate as a cell company pays an additional application fee of $8,000 for the cell company plus $1,000 for each cell (Rule 3.11.5). After permission is granted, each later cell has an application fee of $1,000 (Rule 3.11.7). The application is not treated as submitted until the fee is paid in full (Rule 1.2.1). ADGM Registration Authority commercial-license fees are not set in the captive fee rules.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual supervision fee of $5,000 for either or both of effecting and carrying out contracts of insurance as a captive insurer or an insurance special purpose vehicle (Rule 3.11.4). A cell company pays an additional annual supervision fee of $8,000 plus $1,000 for each cell (Rule 3.11.6). The initial annual fee is prorated for the months remaining in the calendar year and is due within 20 business days of the invoice. Later annual fees are due by the later of 31 January or 20 business days after the invoice (Rule 1.2.2).
Annual Filing Requirements
A captive insurer submits its annual regulatory return in writing within four months of its reporting date, using the electronic prudential reporting system (CIB Rule 2.3.7). The return includes the statements the Regulator specifies. The statement by directors is signed before submission by the senior executive officer and by a director who is not the senior executive officer, and a signed hard copy is kept for at least six years. The auditor's report on the annual regulatory return, and any actuarial report prepared at the reporting date, are submitted within four months of the reporting date. The annual return, including the statement by directors, is subject to audit except where Chapter 2 or the form instructions say a form is not. Returns are in English. A cell company completes a global return and a cell return for each captive cell. Where the Regulator requires a quarterly regulatory return, it is submitted in the manner and within the time the Regulator prescribes. A captive cell that relies on an approved recourse agreement may be required to report cellular and non-cellular resources on an aggregated basis. A captive also reports group capital adequacy under PIN 6.7. Any report submitted to shareholders or policyholders for the reporting period is submitted with the annual return or as soon as practicable afterward. Assets and liabilities are recognised under IFRS except where the Rules provide a different method (Rule 6.3). A captive may outsource managerial functions only to an authorised captive insurance manager, subject to GEN 3.3 (Rule 5.2). Management systems and controls are those in GEN 3. If the captive knows or has reasonable grounds to believe that it is in breach, or may be about to be in breach, of the capital chapter, it tells the Regulator orally within one business day, confirms in writing by the next business day, and makes no distribution to shareholders or members without the Regulator's written permission. The Rules do not state an in-ADGM board-meeting requirement in the sections reviewed.
