Arkansas

Arkansas licenses captive insurers under Arkansas Code Title 23, Chapter 63, Subchapter 16, enacted by Act 1391 of 2001 and administered by the Arkansas Insurance Department. Act 554 of 2025, Senate Bill 237, became Act 554 on 14 April 2025. The Department's Bulletin 9-2025 states that an act of the 2025 regular session without an emergency clause or another stated effective date took effect on 5 August 2025. Act 554 removed the requirement that an association have been in continuous existence for at least one year, authorized a provisional licence, lowered several capital and surplus floors, set a seven-year examination cycle for a pure captive, and added a premium-tax credit for qualifying Arkansas wages. Unimpaired paid-in capital is $300,000 for a producer reinsurance captive, $100,000 for a pure captive, $250,000 for an association or industrial insured captive incorporated as a stock insurer, $100,000 for a sponsored captive, and, for a special purpose captive, an amount the commissioner determines after reviewing the business plan but not less than $125,000. Unimpaired surplus is $300,000 for a producer reinsurance captive, $100,000 for a pure captive, $250,000 for an association or industrial insured stock captive, $500,000 for an association or industrial insured mutual captive, $100,000 for a sponsored captive, and not less than $125,000 for a special purpose captive. The Department publishes the same dollar floors. Direct premium tax is 0.250 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.050 percent on successive bands, and assumed-reinsurance tax is 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent. The annual minimum is $5,000 and the annual maximum is $100,000. The application fee is $200 and the licence and annual renewal fee is $300. A dormant captive maintains at least $25,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus and is not liable for the premium tax. The Department does not publish a captive census, a staff count, or a standard number of days for an ordinary licence. The published contact is the Finance Division at Insurance.Finance@Arkansas.gov.

Tax Environment
Section 23-63-1614, as restated by Act 554 of 2025, requires the tax by 1 March. Direct premiums, after return premiums and dividends on unabsorbed premiums or premium deposits returned or credited to policyholders, are taxed at 0.250 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.050 percent thereafter. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, 0.050 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.025 percent thereafter. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer and to renew or maintain the business with the captive. If the calculated tax is under $5,000, the tax is $5,000. The total tax may not exceed $100,000 in any year. The Department's premium-tax page states the same rates, minimum, and maximum. A captive may apply the noncommissioned salaries and wages of its Arkansas employees, paid in connection with captive operations, as a credit against the tax. The employee must be employed for six months, have a primary residence in Arkansas, and pay Arkansas income tax. The credit may not reduce the tax by more than 50 percent. Two or more captives under common ownership and control are taxed as one captive. For stock corporations that means direct or indirect ownership of 80 percent or more of the outstanding voting stock. For mutual corporations it means direct or indirect ownership of 80 percent or more of the surplus and the voting power. Branch tax applies only to branch business. The tax is all of the tax collectible under Arkansas law from a captive. No other tax may be levied by the state or a county, city, or municipality, except ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used in the production of income. A producer reinsurance captive that invests and continuously maintains at least 50 percent of its assets in qualifying Arkansas bank certificates of deposit, or in qualifying obligations of the state or of an Arkansas county, city, school district, taxing district, or local improvement district, is not subject to this section. A dormant captive is not liable for the tax. A foreign or alien insurer that redomesticates is liable only for tax on premiums paid after redomestication. It may elect to forgo payment in either its first or its second year of operations, but not both. If it then surrenders its licence or redomesticates away within five years, it immediately pays the forgone tax plus 10 percent a year from the original due date. A redomesticating insurer licensed after 1 July is subject to only one-half of the minimum tax in its first year. The Department requires premium tax and fees to be filed and paid through OPTins.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive Unimpaired paid-in capital of not less than $100,000 (section 23-63-1604(a)(1)(B)) and unimpaired surplus of not less than $100,000 (section 23-63-1605(a)(1)(B), reduced from $150,000 by Act 554). The Department's capital table publishes both floors at $100,000.
Association captive An association captive incorporated as a stock insurer maintains unimpaired paid-in capital of not less than $250,000 (section 23-63-1604(a)(1)(C), reduced from $400,000 by Act 554) and unimpaired surplus of not less than $250,000 (section 23-63-1605(a)(1)(C), reduced from $350,000). An association captive incorporated as a mutual insurer maintains unimpaired surplus of not less than $500,000 (section 23-63-1605(a)(1)(E), reduced from $750,000). Section 23-63-1604(a)(1) does not state a separate paid-in capital floor for a mutual association captive. The Department's table lists association capital at $250,000 and association surplus at $250,000 for a stock insurer and $500,000 for a mutual insurer. Act 554 removed the requirement that an association have been in continuous existence for at least one year.
Industrial insured captive An industrial insured captive incorporated as a stock insurer maintains unimpaired paid-in capital of not less than $250,000 (section 23-63-1604(a)(1)(D), raised from $200,000 by Act 554) and unimpaired surplus of not less than $250,000 (section 23-63-1605(a)(1)(D), reduced from $300,000). An industrial insured captive incorporated as a mutual insurer maintains unimpaired surplus of not less than $500,000 (section 23-63-1605(a)(1)(F)). Section 23-63-1604(a)(1) does not state a separate paid-in capital floor for a mutual industrial insured captive. The Department's table lists industrial insured capital at $250,000 and surplus at $250,000 for a stock insurer and $500,000 for a mutual insurer. The Department describes this captive as insuring the risks of the industrial insureds of an industrial insured group or of a risk retention group.
Sponsored captive Unimpaired paid-in capital of not less than $100,000 and unimpaired surplus of not less than $100,000 (sections 23-63-1604(a)(1)(E) and 23-63-1605(a)(1)(G), each reduced from $250,000 by Act 554). The Department publishes both floors at $100,000. The statute and the Department do not state a separate dollar floor for an individual protected cell.
Producer reinsurance captive Unimpaired paid-in capital of not less than $300,000 and unimpaired surplus of not less than $300,000 (sections 23-63-1604(a)(1)(A) and 23-63-1605(a)(1)(A)). Act 554 did not change these amounts. The Department describes this captive as wholly owned by a resident licensed insurance producer, acting only as a reinsurer for risks written by or placed through its parent or an affiliate, and permitted to establish protected cells.
Special purpose captive Capital and surplus are each an amount determined by the commissioner after considering the business plan, feasibility study, and pro formas, including the nature of the risks, but not less than $125,000 (sections 23-63-1604(a)(1)(F) and 23-63-1605(a)(1)(H), each reduced from $300,000 by Act 554). The Department's table publishes $125,000 for both.
Branch captive The Department describes a branch captive as licensed to transact insurance through a business unit with a principal place of business in Arkansas and as operating as a pure captive with respect to those operations. Section 23-63-1604(c) requires a trust fund in the United States, funded by an irrevocable letter of credit or another acceptable asset, for the benefit of United States policyholders and United States ceding insurers. The security may be no less than the capital and surplus required by the subchapter plus the reserves on the branch policies and contracts, including loss reserves, allocated loss-adjustment expenses, incurred-but-not-reported losses, and unearned premiums. The Department's capital table does not state a separate branch dollar floor.
Dormant captive A dormant captive that has been issued a certificate of dormancy possesses and maintains unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus of not less than $25,000 (section 23-63-1624(c)(1)). The section applies to a pure, sponsored, or industrial insured captive that has ceased transacting insurance and has no remaining insurance liabilities. The Department's capital table does not restate this amount.
Form and additional capital Capital may be cash, other assets acceptable to the commissioner, or an irrevocable letter of credit issued by an Arkansas-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank and approved by the commissioner (section 23-63-1604(a)(2)). The commissioner may prescribe additional capital based on the type, volume, and nature of the business (section 23-63-1604(b)). Capital stock of a captive incorporated as a stock insurer is issued at not less than par value.

Regulatory Framework

Arkansas Code Title 23, Chapter 63, Subchapter 16, Licensing and Regulation of Captive Insurers, and 23 CAR Part 29

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Subchapter 16 was enacted by Act 1391 of 2001, House Bill 2502. Capital is section 23-63-1604 and surplus is section 23-63-1605, both amended by Act 554 of 2025. Reporting is section 23-63-1607. Examinations are section 23-63-1608, with the pure-captive cycle added by Act 554. Premium tax is section 23-63-1614, as restated by Act 554. Dormant captives are section 23-63-1624, added by Act 370 of 2017. Act 554 amended only the renewal-fee cross-reference in that section and left the $25,000 capital-and-surplus floor in place. Reporting on generally accepted accounting principles, unless the commissioner approves statutory accounting principles, is section 23-63-1607(b) as amended by Act 521 of 2019. The general examination cycle of at least once every five years is section 23-63-1608(a) as amended by Act 469 of 2023. Act 554 added the seven-year cycle for a pure captive. Application, licence, and renewal fees are 23 CAR section 29-117. The captive rule was effective 12 February 2002 and is now codified as 23 CAR Part 29. Act 554 became Act 554 on 14 April 2025. Bulletin 9-2025 states that 2025 acts without an emergency clause or another stated date took effect on 5 August 2025. Act 554 contains no emergency clause.

Licensing Speed
Act 554 of 2025 allows the commissioner to issue a provisional licence when the commissioner finds that doing so is in the public interest, after a complete application, payment of all licence fees, and a preliminary finding that the expertise, experience, and character of the persons who will control and manage the applicant are acceptable. The commissioner may limit or revoke the provisional licence. It terminates automatically if the applicant does not complete the regular licensure process. The Act does not state a maximum number of days. The Department describes the ordinary process as quick and efficient and does not publish a standard turnaround.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.250 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.050 percent thereafter. Assumed reinsurance: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $20 million bands. Annual minimum $5,000. Annual maximum $100,000. Due 1 March. A wage credit for qualifying Arkansas employees may offset up to 50 percent of the tax. A qualifying producer reinsurance captive is outside the section. A dormant captive is not liable for the tax. The tax is in lieu of other state and local taxes except ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used to produce income.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
23 CAR section 29-117 requires $200 for filing and review of each application, $300 for the licence when issued, $300 for annual renewal, and an actuarial review fee equal to the cost to the Department. The Department's application instructions state the same non-refundable $200 filing fee, payable to the Arkansas Insurance Department Trust Fund, and state that a $300 fee for the original certificate of authority is requested if the application is approved. Secretary of State organization fees are not set in Part 29 or Subchapter 16.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual licence renewal of $300 under 23 CAR section 29-117. A dormant captive pays the licence renewal fee provided in that section. Its certificate of dormancy is renewed every five years. Premium tax and fees are filed and paid through OPTins. The Department's captive pages do not publish a separate dormancy-fee amount.
Annual Filing Requirements
Before 1 March of each year, or within an extension the commissioner grants for good cause, a captive submits a report of its financial condition verified by the oath of two executive officers. Act 521 of 2019 states that, except as sections 23-63-1604 and 23-63-1605 provide, a captive reports using generally accepted accounting principles unless the commissioner approves statutory accounting principles, with any modifications the commissioner requires. An association captive and an industrial insured group file in the form required by section 23-63-216(a). The Department's filing table assigns quarterly and annual statements to association captives and industrial insured groups, and annual statements only to industrial insured, pure, sponsored, and producer reinsurance captives. A producer reinsurance captive or a pure captive may apply to report on a fiscal year consistent with its parent. The Department states that the report is then due 60 days after fiscal year-end. To support the premium-tax return, the Department's premium-tax page instructs a pure captive to file, before 1 March, pages 1, 2, 3, and 8 of the Captive Annual Statement: Pure or Industrial Insured, verified by two executive officers. 23 CAR section 29-103 requires an annual audit by an independent certified public accountant authorized by the commissioner, filed by 30 June for the preceding calendar year, on the same accounting basis as the annual statement. The audit includes an opinion on the adequacy of loss and loss-expense reserves by a person the commissioner approves who is a Fellow of the Casualty Actuarial Society or a member in good standing of the American Academy of Actuaries. Act 554 allows the commissioner to waive the audit or actuarial opinion for a pure captive whose parent has a consolidated audit, a guaranty of the pure captive's liabilities, minimum net equity of $100 million, and a financial-strength rating of BBB or better from a rating agency the commissioner accepts. A dormant captive files a verified financial-condition report before 15 March. The captive holds at least one board meeting, or a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee meeting, each year in Arkansas. It maintains its registered office in Arkansas, or the branch office for a branch captive, and appoints a resident registered agent. At least one director of a corporate captive, and at least one member of a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee, is a resident of the United States or a United States territory. Act 469 of 2023 requires an examination at least once every five years, or whenever the commissioner determines it to be prudent, at the company's expense. Act 554 provides that a pure captive is examined at least once every seven years or whenever the commissioner determines it to be prudent. A branch examination is limited to branch business and branch operations if the branch annually provides a certificate of compliance from the jurisdiction where it is formed and demonstrates that it is operating in sound financial condition under that jurisdiction's law. A captive may not provide personal motor vehicle or homeowners insurance or any component of those coverages. It may not accept or cede reinsurance except as authorized by section 23-63-1611. It is not permitted to join or contribute to a state guaranty or insolvency fund or to receive benefits from one. The Department states that pure captives and industrial insured captives are not subject to any restrictions on allowable investments contained in the Arkansas Insurance Code. Act 521 of 2019 requires a sponsored captive to comply with the investment requirements in section 23-63-801, except to the extent credit for reinsurance is allowed under section 23-63-1611 or the commissioner finds another extent reasonable. The same act allows the assets of two or more protected cells to be combined for investment without defeating segregation for accounting or other purposes. It also states that a risk retention group formed under the Risk Retention and Purchasing Groups Act and subject to Subchapter 16 is subject to additional specified provisions of the Insurance Code. The Department's type list does not publish a separate capital floor for that risk retention group.
