Texas

Texas licenses captive insurance companies under Insurance Code Chapter 964, added in 2013 by Senate Bill 734 and effective 14 June 2013. A 2017 amendment authorized captive exchanges. A captive may write direct insurance and reinsurance, but only for the operational risks of its affiliates and the risks of a controlled unaffiliated business. It may be formed in any form of organization authorized by the Business Organizations Code except a risk retention group or a general partnership, and it may be a nonprofit corporation only if a nonprofit corporation controls it. A captive exchange is a reciprocal or interinsurance exchange and operates as a captive insurance company. The commissioner sets unencumbered capital and surplus, or the equivalent, at not less than $250,000. Premium tax under Chapter 223A is one-half of one percent of taxable premium receipts, with a $7,500 annual minimum and a $200,000 annual maximum. Premiums received from another authorized insurer for reinsurance are not taxed, and there is no deduction for premiums paid for reinsurance. Captives also pay the insurance maintenance tax on correctly reported gross premiums from risks located in Texas. The application fee is $1,500. Affiliates must have significant operations in Texas, the governing body meets at least once a year in the state, and the principal office and books and records stay in Texas unless the commissioner approves a move. TDI's 15 April 2026 post reported that the sector grew 8.6 percent to 101 state-domiciled captives in the six months leading up to April 2026, and quoted the captive specialist that Texas has only one type of captive. The fiscal 2025 annual report counted 93 captive insurance company licenses. No formation, surrender, or type split was published with the figure of 101.

Tax Environment
Insurance Code section 223A.003 imposes an annual tax of one-half of one percent of a captive insurance company's taxable premium receipts. Taxable receipts include gross premiums, membership fees, assessments, dues, revenues, and other considerations for any kind of insurance written on any kind of property or risk, without regard to where the property or risk is located. They do not include premiums received from another authorized insurer for reinsurance, returned premiums, dividends paid to policyholders, or premiums excluded by another Texas law. There is no deduction for premiums the captive pays for reinsurance. The annual minimum aggregate tax is $7,500 and the annual maximum aggregate tax is $200,000. The tax is due not later than 1 March. A captive whose net tax liability for the previous calendar year exceeded $1,000 makes semiannual prepayments on 1 March and 1 August. A credit is allowed for examination and evaluation fees paid to the state during the year, within the limits in section 803.007. Gross premiums taxed under Chapter 223A are not subject to other taxes, surcharges, or regulatory assessments or fees under the Insurance Code except insurance maintenance taxes under section 964.068. That maintenance tax applies to correctly reported gross premiums from insurance on risks located in Texas, at the line-of-business rates the commissioner sets. The Comptroller states the same 0.5 percent rate, $7,500 minimum, and $200,000 maximum. A single captive maintenance-tax percentage was not published.

Regulatory Framework

Texas Insurance Code Chapter 964 (Captive Insurance Companies) and Chapter 223A (Captive Insurance Premium Tax)

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Texas Insurance Code Chapter 964, added by Acts 2013, 83rd Legislature, Regular Session, Chapter 569 (Senate Bill 734), effective 14 June 2013, and amended by Acts 2017, 85th Legislature, Regular Session, Chapter 1050 (House Bill 1944), effective 15 June 2017, including the captive-exchange subchapter. Capital and surplus are section 964.056. The application and the $1,500 application fee are section 964.057. The annual report is section 964.060. The maintenance tax is section 964.068. Premium tax is Chapter 223A, added by the same 2013 act. Captive administrative rules are Title 28, Texas Administrative Code, Chapter 6. The Comptroller's captive premium-tax rule is 34 Texas Administrative Code section 3.827.

Premium Tax Rate
0.5 percent of taxable premium receipts. Annual minimum $7,500 and annual maximum $200,000. Due 1 March, with semiannual prepayments on 1 March and 1 August when the prior year's net tax liability exceeded $1,000. Reinsurance premiums received from another authorized insurer are excluded, and premiums paid for reinsurance are not deductible. Maintenance tax under section 964.068 is additional and is set by line of business.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Application fee of $1,500, or a greater amount the commissioner sets by rule to recover the cost of administering section 964.057 (Texas Insurance Code section 964.057(c)). TDI's captive page uses the certificate of authority application FIN180 and the company licensing fee transmittal FIN321. No greater fee was found in the materials reviewed. Secretary of State formation fees are separate and are not set in Chapter 964.
Annual Maintenance Fee
No separate annual captive certificate-of-authority renewal fee is stated in Chapter 964. The annual charges are the Chapter 223A premium tax and the section 964.068 maintenance tax on Texas risks.
Annual Filing Requirements
On or before 1 March, a captive files a statement of financial condition verified by two executive officers, in the format the commissioner prescribes. On or before 1 June, it files a year-end report of its financial condition with an independent certified public accountant's opinion (Texas Insurance Code section 964.060). The commissioner may allow a fiscal year-end: the annual report is then due not later than the 60th day after fiscal year-end, the audited report not later than the 150th day after the annual report is due, and a balance sheet, income statement, and statement of cash flows verified by two executive officers is still filed before 1 March to support the premium-tax return. The commissioner may waive the actuarial report if net written premium or reinsurance assumed is less than $1 million, or if the company had been in operation for less than six months at the end of the previous calendar year. TDI's captive filing instructions also require the annual statement in Excel, an actuarial opinion or waiver affidavit, and financial projections every third year, and they point captive insurers that are part of a holding-company system to Insurance Code Chapter 823. The Chapter 223A premium-tax report is due on the 1 March tax due date. The governing body holds at least one meeting each year in Texas (section 964.055).
