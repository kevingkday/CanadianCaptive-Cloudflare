France

France has no separate captive insurance statute. Article L.350-2 of the Code des assurances, created by Ordinance No. 2015-378 of 2 April 2015 and in force since 1 January 2016, defines an entreprise captive d'assurance and an entreprise captive de réassurance. Both are authorized and supervised by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution as insurance or reinsurance undertakings under the Code des assurances and Solvency II. A captive insurer's object is exclusively the insurance of risks of the undertaking or undertakings to which it belongs, or of other undertakings in its group. A captive reinsurer's object is the same cover in reinsurance, and its head office is in France. The absolute floor of the minimum capital requirement, set by Article A.352-29 since 29 October 2022, is EUR 2,700,000 for non-life insurance, including a captive insurer, EUR 4,000,000 where classes 10 to 15 are covered and for life insurance, EUR 3,900,000 for a reinsurer, and EUR 1,300,000 for a captive reinsurer. The minimum capital requirement must also lie between 25 and 45 percent of the solvency capital requirement. Article 6 of the Finance Law for 2023, codified as Article 39 quinquies G, II, of the General Tax Code, lets an eligible captive reinsurer deduct a provision pour résilience. The annual allocation is limited to 90 percent of the relevant technical result, and the stock of the provision may not exceed ten times the average minimum capital requirement over three years. The ordinary corporate income-tax rate is 25 percent. The ACPR's captive-reinsurance guide, published on 12 November 2024, said that 17 captive reinsurers had already been authorized. That is the latest official count found. It is not a later census of licences in force, and it does not count captive insurers. The guide tells applicants to write to the Authorizations Directorate at 2785-secretariat-direction-ut@acpr.banque-france.fr. Once a file is complete, silence for six months is an implicit refusal under Articles R.321-4 and R.321-5-3.

Tax Environment
Captive insurers and reinsurers are subject to corporate income tax. Article 219, I, of the General Tax Code, in force as amended on 21 February 2026, sets the normal rate at 25 percent. Article 219, I, b, sets a 15 percent rate, for financial years opened from 1 January 2002, on taxable profit up to EUR 42,500 in a twelve-month period, where turnover does not exceed EUR 10 million. That reduced rate also requires the company's capital to be fully paid up and held continuously, at least 75 percent, by natural persons or by a company that itself meets those conditions. A captive owned by a corporate group will commonly fail that holding test. The captive-specific relief is the provision pour résilience in Article 39 quinquies G, II. It is available to a captive reinsurer under Article L.350-2, 3°, that is owned by an undertaking other than a financial undertaking within Article L.310-3, 12°, and whose object is exclusively the reinsurance of risks of undertakings that are not financial undertakings. The provision covers accepted reinsurance of the categories listed in Article A.344-2 as in force on 31 December 2022: professional and agricultural property damage, natural catastrophes, general liability, pecuniary loss, damage and pecuniary loss from attacks on information and communication systems, and transport. Article 16 A, II, of Annex II limits the annual allocation to 90 percent of the sum of the technical profits of those categories, and limits the total provision to ten times the average minimum capital requirement over the last three years. Unused annual allocations are brought back into taxable profit in the sixteenth year. Risks reserved under paragraph II cannot also be reserved under paragraph I. The provision is recognized under Article R.343-8, 12° bis. The decree of 7 June 2024 applies the Annex II limits to financial years opened from 1 January 2024. No separate insurance-premium-tax rate for a captive was extracted from these articles. The ACPR guide states that supervised undertakings pay an annual contribution for supervision costs. It says Article L.612-20, II, B, of the Monetary and Financial Code bases that contribution on premiums issued and accepted in the last closed year, and that Article L.612-20, III, 2°, sets a rate between 0.15 and 0.25 per thousand and a minimum between EUR 500 and EUR 1,500. The rate fixed for a particular year inside that band was not extracted.

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Captive reinsurance undertaking Absolute floor of EUR 1,300,000 (Article A.352-29, point iii). The ACPR authorization note states the same EUR 1,300,000 floor for a captive reinsurer and EUR 3,900,000 for other reinsurers.
How the minimum is set Article R.352-29 sets the minimum capital requirement as an amount of eligible basic own funds below which policyholders and cedants would face an unacceptable risk if the undertaking continued. The linear function is calibrated to an 85 percent value-at-risk of basic own funds over one year. Without displacing the absolute floor, the minimum capital requirement must lie between 25 and 45 percent of the solvency capital requirement, including any capital add-on under Article L.352-3. It is calculated at least quarterly and reported to the ACPR. The euro floors are fixed by order under Article A.352-29.
Non-life insurance, including a captive insurer Absolute floor of EUR 2,700,000 (Article A.352-29, point i). If any of the risks in classes 10 to 15 of Article R.321-1 is covered, the floor may not be lower than the life floor in point ii.
Life insurance, including a captive insurer Absolute floor of EUR 4,000,000 (Article A.352-29, point ii).
Composite insurance The floor is the sum of the non-life and life floors (Article R.352-29, I, d, iv).
Reinsurance undertaking Absolute floor of EUR 3,900,000 (Article A.352-29, point iii).

Regulatory Framework

Code des assurances, including Articles L.350-2, L.321-1, L.321-1-1, R.321-4, R.321-5-3, R.352-29, and A.352-29, and Article 39 quinquies G of the Code général des impôts

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Article L.350-2 was created by Ordinance No. 2015-378 of 2 April 2015 and has been in force since 1 January 2016. It defines entreprise captive d'assurance and entreprise captive de réassurance. Authorization of insurance undertakings is Article L.321-1, and authorization of reinsurance undertakings is Article L.321-1-1. The six-month implicit refusal is Article R.321-4 for insurance and Article R.321-5-3 for reinsurance. The minimum capital requirement is Article R.352-29, as amended by Decree No. 2022-1018 of 20 July 2022, in force on 1 October 2022. The euro floors are Article A.352-29, created by the order of 21 October 2022 and in force on 29 October 2022. The ACPR states the same floors in its authorization guidance, citing Articles R.352-29 and A.352-29. The provision pour résilience is Article 39 quinquies G, II, of the Code général des impôts, introduced by Article 6 of Law No. 2022-1726 of 30 December 2022 (Finance Law for 2023) and amended by Article 4 of Law No. 2023-1322 of 29 December 2023. The 90 percent and ten-times-MCR limits are Article 16 A, II, of Annex II to the General Tax Code. Accounting recognition is Article R.343-8, 12° bis, of the Code des assurances. Corporate income tax is Article 219 of the General Tax Code, whose normal rate is 25 percent. The ACPR contribution for supervision costs is described, by reference to Article L.612-20 of the Monetary and Financial Code, in the ACPR captive-reinsurance guide of 12 November 2024.

Licensing Speed
The ACPR captive-reinsurance guide states that, once the file is complete, the ACPR has a regulatory period of six months to decide. Completeness is the transmission, in usable form, of every document required by the annexes to Instruction 2015-I-15. Article R.321-4, for an insurance undertaking, and Article R.321-5-3, for a reinsurance undertaking, provide that the absence of a notification at the end of six months from receipt of a duly completed application is an implicit refusal. The six months are extended if the ACPR defers the decision under Article L.321-2. Authorization is not automatic. The guide says the decision is taken by the ACPR's Supervisory College.
Premium Tax Rate
No captive premium-tax rate was extracted from the articles reviewed. Corporate income tax is 25 percent under Article 219, I. The 15 percent rate on the first EUR 42,500 applies only where turnover does not exceed EUR 10 million and the fully paid-up capital is held at least 75 percent by natural persons, or by a company meeting the same test. Eligible captive reinsurers may deduct the provision pour résilience, capped each year at 90 percent of the relevant technical result and in total at ten times the three-year average minimum capital requirement.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
No fixed captive application fee is stated in the Code des assurances articles or the ACPR guide reviewed. The guide says a project is first presented to the Authorizations Directorate and the complete file is then filed through the ACPR portal.
Annual Maintenance Fee
The ACPR guide says every supervised undertaking pays an annual contribution for supervision costs. It describes the base as premiums issued and accepted in the last closed financial year, under Article L.612-20, II, B, of the Monetary and Financial Code, and a rate between 0.15 and 0.25 per thousand, with a minimum between EUR 500 and EUR 1,500, under Article L.612-20, III, 2°. The order setting the rate for a given year inside that band was not extracted.
Annual Filing Requirements
After authorization, the ACPR guide says a captive reinsurer is subject to the Solvency II reporting obligations, filed from the first year through the OneGate portal. Article R.352-29, IV, requires the minimum capital requirement to be calculated at least quarterly and transmitted to the ACPR. The guide says a change of direct or indirect ownership that crosses a threshold upward, and keeping a financial year-end other than 31 December beyond the first year unless the authorization already allowed it, need prior ACPR approval. Changes of bylaws, appointment or renewal of an effective manager or a key-function holder, a downward ownership-threshold crossing, and a change of provider of critical or important activities must be notified, and the ACPR may object. The guide requires at least two effective managers, so that the four-eyes principle is met, and an identified holder of each of the four key functions: actuarial, risk management, compliance, and internal audit. Proportionality may allow one person to combine roles, except that internal audit is not combined except in the cases the ACPR notice describes, and one person may not hold more than three functions. The ACPR may withdraw the authorization if the conditions for it are no longer met, and it may record that the authorization has lapsed if it is unused. A standard number of days for the annual quantitative templates, beyond the quarterly minimum-capital transmission, was not extracted from the national reporting page linked in the guide.
