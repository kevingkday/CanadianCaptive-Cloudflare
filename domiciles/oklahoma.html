Oklahoma

Oklahoma licenses captive insurers under the Oklahoma Captive Insurance Company Act, Title 36, sections 6470.1 through 6470.35, first enacted as Chapter 334, Oklahoma Session Laws 2004, and administered by the Captive Insurance Division of the Oklahoma Insurance Department. A pure captive holds at least $250,000, of which $150,000 is paid in before the license is issued and the remaining $100,000 is paid in by the first anniversary. An association captive incorporated as a stock insurer holds at least $750,000, an industrial insured captive incorporated as a stock insurer at least $500,000, a sponsored captive at least $500,000, and a risk retention group at least $1,000,000. A special purpose or branch captive holds at least $250,000 or another amount the commissioner sets. A series captive's capital is an amount the commissioner specifies. Direct premium tax is 0.2 percent and assumed-reinsurance premium tax is 0.1 percent, due 1 March. The ordinary minimum is $5,000 and the maximum is $100,000. A series captive's annual minimum is $3,500. The nonrefundable application fee is $200, or $300 for a special purpose captive, and the license and renewal fee is $300. The Division states that it will process a completed license application within 30 to 45 business days. The commissioner may issue a provisional license. The Department's 2025 annual report states that 2024 ended with 64 captive insurers, after a net gain of five. The 19 February 2026 release states that in 2025 the Department approved all 14 applications it received, four captives dissolved, and the net gain was 10, surpassing the 2017 high of 73. That release does not state the 31 December 2025 headcount. There is no in-state manager, in-state board member, or in-state annual board-meeting requirement. Applications and questions go to oidcaptive@oid.ok.gov. Steve Kinion is Director of the Captive Insurance Division. No current staff count is published.

Tax Environment
Section 6470.19, as restated by Senate Bill 1035, taxes each captive other than a sponsored captive, and each protected cell of a sponsored captive, at 0.2 percent of direct premiums after return premiums and policyholder dividends or premium deposits returned or credited. Annuity considerations are not taxed. Assumed-reinsurance premium is taxed at 0.1 percent. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer. A sponsored captive pays, in the aggregate, the minimum tax. If the calculated tax is under $5,000, the tax is $5,000. In the year a captive is first licensed, or a protected cell is first approved, that minimum is prorated by quarter: $5,000, $3,750, $2,500, or $1,250. A series captive pays an annual minimum aggregate tax of $3,500. The tax paid by more than one series formed in one limited liability company or statutory trust is not restricted by the annual maximum. If the calculated tax exceeds $100,000, the tax is $100,000, subject to the common-ownership and employment provisions. Captives under common ownership and control, defined as 80 percent of the voting stock or other voting interests, are taxed as one captive, and protected cells under that ownership are taxed as one cell. A captive that employed 25 or more qualified individuals in Oklahoma throughout the tax year, and that would otherwise owe more than $50,000, pays $50,000. A qualified individual is employed in Oklahoma at least 35 hours a week by the captive or by a wholly owned subsidiary that provides management, operating, investment, or related services only to that captive. The tax is in lieu of other Oklahoma occupation taxes, except ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used to produce income. The return is due 1 March. The Department requires the filing through OPTins. A dormant captive is not liable for the section 6470 tax during the initial five-year dormancy (section 6470.35).

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive Not less than $250,000 of unimpaired aggregate paid-in capital and surplus. $150,000 must be paid in before the license is issued, and an additional $100,000 must be paid in on or before the first anniversary (36 O.S. section 6470.6(A)(1), Senate Bill 1035).
Association captive Not less than $750,000 for an association captive incorporated as a stock insurer (section 6470.6(A)(2)).
Industrial insured captive Not less than $500,000 for an industrial insured captive incorporated as a stock insurer (section 6470.6(A)(3)).
Sponsored captive Not less than $500,000 (section 6470.6(A)(4)). The statute does not set a separate dollar floor for an individual protected cell.
Risk retention group Not less than $1,000,000 for a captive doing business as a risk retention group (section 6470.6(A)(5)).
Special purpose or branch captive Not less than $250,000, or an amount determined by the commissioner after considering the business plan, feasibility study, and pro formas, including the nature of the risks to be insured (section 6470.6(A)(6)). The Department's types page states the same standard.
Series captive An amount specified by the commissioner (section 6470.6(A)(7)). The statute does not set a dollar floor. The Department's types page states the same standard.
Dormant captive Not less than $25,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus while a certificate of dormancy is in force (section 6470.35, Senate Bill 1240).
Form of capital Cash, a cash equivalent, or an irrevocable letter of credit issued by an Oklahoma-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank, and approved by the commissioner. The commissioner may prescribe additional capital based on the type, volume, and nature of the business. For a branch captive, the commissioner may require a U.S. trust, funded by an irrevocable letter of credit or other acceptable asset, of not less than the required capital and surplus plus the reserves on branch policies and reinsurance contracts.

Regulatory Framework

Oklahoma Captive Insurance Company Act, Title 36, Oklahoma Statutes, sections 6470.1 through 6470.35, and Oklahoma Administrative Code 365:25-15-1 through 365:25-15-23

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

The Oklahoma Captive Insurance Company Act originated as Chapter 334, Oklahoma Session Laws 2004. Enrolled House Bill 2905 (2006) cites that chapter as the source of the captive sections. Capital and surplus are section 6470.6, as restated by enrolled Senate Bill 1035 (2021). The license, fees, and provisional license are section 6470.3, as restated by enrolled Senate Bill 1242, effective 1 November 2022. Reports are section 6470.11, as restated by Senate Bill 1035. Examinations are section 6470.13, as amended by enrolled House Bill 1108 (2013). Premium tax is section 6470.19, as restated by Senate Bill 1035. Dormancy is section 6470.35, as amended by enrolled Senate Bill 1240, effective 1 November 2022. The Department posts OAC 365:25-15 as its captive rules.

Licensing Speed
The Captive Insurance Division states that it will process a completed license application within 30 to 45 business days, and that it will answer questions by phone or email within 1 to 3 business days. The commissioner may issue a provisional license when the public interest will be served, after a complete application, payment of the licensure fees, and a preliminary finding that the people who will control and manage the captive are acceptable. The Division's homepage describes optional provisional licensure as authority to begin business on the day the application is filed.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.2 percent. Assumed reinsurance premiums: 0.1 percent. Ordinary minimum $5,000, prorated by quarter in the year of first licensure or first cell approval. Series-captive minimum $3,500. Annual maximum $100,000. A captive employing 25 or more qualified Oklahoma individuals pays $50,000 if it would otherwise owe more. Due 1 March through OPTins. A dormant captive owes no section 6470 tax during the initial five years of dormancy.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 6470.3, as restated by Senate Bill 1242, requires a nonrefundable application fee of $200 to review whether the application is complete. A special purpose captive pays a nonrefundable fee of $300. The commissioner may also charge the reasonable cost of outside legal, financial, and examination services. The license fee for the year of registration is $300. The formation page states that the filing includes a $200 review fee and a $300 license fee. A provisional license requires the same fees. Organization fees payable to the Oklahoma Secretary of State are not set in the Captive Insurance Company Act.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual license renewal fee of $300 (section 6470.3). The license authorizes business until the succeeding 1 March, when it may be renewed. A dormant captive pays a nonrefundable annual fee of $500 (section 6470.35) rather than the premium tax during the initial five-year dormancy. The certificate of dormancy is renewed every five years and is forfeited if it is not renewed.
Annual Filing Requirements
Before 1 March, a captive submits a report of its financial condition verified by two executive officers (section 6470.11). Reporting uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the commissioner approves statutory accounting principles or international accounting standards. A pure captive may be allowed to file on its parent's fiscal year. The statute then requires the annual report 60 days after fiscal year-end, and pages 1 through 7 of the Captive Annual Statement for the calendar year-end before 1 March to support the premium-tax return. OAC 365:25-15-3, in the rules PDF the Department posts, states that an approved pure-captive fiscal-year report is filed no later than 180 days after fiscal year-end. The audited financial report is due 30 June for a 31 December year-end. The same rule says a pure captive approved to report on a fiscal year files that report no later than 180 days after fiscal year-end. The commissioner may exempt a company with direct written and assumed premiums of $2,000,000 or less in the preceding year from the annual audit, and may exempt a company at that premium level from the annual actuarial opinion. The actuarial opinion certifies life, health, or annuity reserves, or loss and loss-expense reserves, and is given by a member of the American Academy of Actuaries or the Casualty Actuarial Society who meets the U.S. qualification standards, or by another person who has demonstrated competence in loss-reserve evaluation. A risk retention group files the report required by Title 36, section 311. Premium tax is filed through OPTins and is due 1 March. The Department states there is no in-state manager, in-state board member, or in-state annual board-meeting requirement. A captive maintains a place of business in Oklahoma designated as its registered office and appoints a resident registered agent. Section 6470.13, as amended by House Bill 1108, requires an examination at least once in five years, which the commissioner may enlarge to seven years if the captive is subject to a comprehensive annual audit of a scope the commissioner accepts, by independent auditors the commissioner approves. A dormant captive files a financial-condition report by 1 March. The rules PDF the Department still links, last amended effective 1 November 2021, describes an earlier dormancy tax. The profile follows section 6470.35 as amended in 2022.
