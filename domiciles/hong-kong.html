Hong Kong

Hong Kong authorises captive insurers under the Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41), section 2(7). A captive is authorised for general business only. That business must not cover liabilities or risks that an Ordinance requires a person to insure, and it is limited to risks of bodies corporate in the captive's corporate group, the proportional share of another body's risks to which the captive or a first-tier group member is directly exposed, and other risks that the captive or a first-tier member controls, oversees, manages, or is otherwise sufficiently connected to. The Insurance (Amendment) Ordinance 2023 and the subsidiary legislation for the risk-based capital regime commenced on 1 July 2024. Captive capital is set by the Insurance (Marine Insurers and Captive Insurers) Rules (Cap. 41U), not by the general Insurance (Valuation and Capital) Rules. The capital base must be at least HK$2 million and at least the prescribed capital amount. The Insurance Authority's list names nine authorised captive insurers as at 6 July 2026. The Authority's 2024-25 annual report said two new captive authorisations had been granted up to August 2025. An eligible captive may elect a concessionary profits tax rate of one-half of the Schedule 8 rate. The Inland Revenue Department states the normal corporate rate as 16.5 percent, so the concessionary rate is 8.25 percent, on qualifying captive insurance profits for the year of assessment 2018/19 onwards. Profits on which that election is made do not also receive the two-tiered rates. The Authority's captive brochure, published in March 2026, gives the captive contact as captives@ia.org.hk and says processing takes approximately three months.

Tax Environment
The Inland Revenue Department states the normal profits tax rate for a corporation, from the year of assessment 2008/09, as 16.5 percent. From the year of assessment 2018/19, two-tiered rates charge 8.25 percent on the first HK$2 million of assessable profits and 16.5 percent above that. A concessionary rate of 50 percent of the normal profits tax rate applies to qualifying profits of an authorised captive insurer from the year of assessment 2018/19. The Department's profits-tax-return notes say the concessionary rate is one-half of the rate in Schedule 8 to the Inland Revenue Ordinance, which is the 8.25 percent rate the Authority states, and that it applies only if the captive elects. Profits taxed at the concessionary rate do not also qualify for the two-tiered rates. An authorised captive insurer files supplementary form S7 with the profits tax return whether or not it claims the concession, including where it has a loss. The Authority says the practical benefit may differ under the OECD base-erosion and profit-shifting reform, depending on the group's effective tax rate in Hong Kong, and refers readers to the Inland Revenue Department. The captive requirements page does not state a separate premium tax. The Authority's funding includes a policyholder levy of 0.1 percent of premium, capped at HK$100 a year for a long-term policy and HK$5,000 a year for a general policy. The captive page does not say whether that levy is collected on a captive policy.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Captive insurer, floor The capital base must also be at least HK$2 million, even where the premium and claims calculation would be lower, and at least the minimum capital amount.
Captive insurer, prescribed capital amount Under rule 8, Table 2, of Legal Notice 65 of 2024, if the greater of net premium for the 12 months ending on the valuation date and relevant claims outstanding does not exceed HK$40 million or its equivalent, the prescribed capital amount for general business is HK$2 million or its equivalent. If that greater amount exceeds HK$40 million, the prescribed capital amount is 5 percent of the net premium or 5 percent of the relevant claims outstanding, whichever is the greater.
How the minimum is set Rule 4 of the Insurance (Marine Insurers and Captive Insurers) Rules requires the capital base to be at least each of the prescribed capital amount, the minimum capital amount, and HK$2 million. The minimum capital amount is the same as the prescribed capital amount unless the Authority varies or relaxes it. The capital base is Unlimited Tier 1 capital, plus Limited Tier 1 capital of no more than 10 percent of the prescribed capital amount, plus Tier 2 capital of no more than 50 percent of the prescribed capital amount. Guideline on Valuation and Capital Requirements (GL36), in effect from 1 July 2025, states the same HK$2 million floor for a marine insurer or a captive insurer, against HK$20 million for an applicable insurer. The Authority's captive requirements page states the same HK$2 million floor and says the prescribed capital amount uses a calculation based on net premium or relevant claims outstanding.
Other authorised insurers An insurer other than a marine insurer, a captive insurer, a special purpose insurer, or Lloyd's must keep a capital base of at least its prescribed capital amount, its minimum capital amount, and HK$20 million. That is not the captive test.

Regulatory Framework

Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41), and the Insurance (Marine Insurers and Captive Insurers) Rules (Cap. 41U)

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41), section 2(7), for the captive definition, and section 8 for authorisation. The Insurance (Amendment) Ordinance 2023 commenced on 1 July 2024 and brought the risk-based capital framework into force with its subsidiary legislation. Insurance (Marine Insurers and Captive Insurers) Rules (Cap. 41U), Legal Notice 65 of 2024, set the captive valuation, capital, and fund requirements from that commencement. Insurance (Exemption to Appointment of Actuary) Rules (Cap. 41Q) exempt captive insurers from appointing an actuary. Insurance (Valuation and Capital) Rules (Cap. 41R) apply to authorised insurers other than marine insurers, captive insurers, and Lloyd's. Insurance (Submission of Statements, Reports and Information) Rules (Cap. 41S) set the reporting framework. Insurance (Authorization and Annual Fees) Regulation (Cap. 41C), Schedule 2, as replaced by Legal Notice 58 of 2017 and retained for captives by Legal Notice 2 of 2021, sets the captive authorisation and annual fees. Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), section 14B(1)(b), is the concession cited by the Inland Revenue Department for an authorised captive insurer. The Companies (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2025 re-domiciliation regime came into operation on 23 May 2025. An authorised insurer incorporated outside Hong Kong that re-domiciles is supervised as if incorporated in Hong Kong, after a letter of no-objection under the new section 3BA(3) of the Insurance Ordinance.

Licensing Speed
The Insurance Authority's captive insurance brochure, published in March 2026, says application processing time is approximately three months, subject to the completeness and quality of the application materials. The brochure describes an initial discussion, a preliminary meeting, an unsigned draft application, a signed formal application, an approval in principle, an office inspection, and then the certificate of authorisation. The application form it names is Form IA-6C, filed by the ultimate parent.
Premium Tax Rate
Concessionary profits tax of one-half of the Schedule 8 corporate rate, stated by the Authority as 8.25 percent, on qualifying profits from authorised captive insurance business, if the captive elects. The normal corporate rate is 16.5 percent. The two-tiered 8.25 percent rate on the first HK$2 million is not available for profits on which the captive concession is elected. No captive premium-tax rate is stated on the captive requirements page.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Schedule 2 to the Insurance (Authorization and Annual Fees) Regulation, replaced by Legal Notice 58 of 2017, charges a captive insurer, on authorisation under section 13(1)(a), the aggregate of a fixed fee of HK$30,000 and a variable fee. Legal Notice 2 of 2021 left captive insurers on that Schedule 2 structure and prescribed a separate fixed fee of HK$15,000, with no variable fee, for a special purpose insurer. The Authority's 2024-25 annual report states the captive fixed fee as HK$30,000. The variable fee for Schedule 2 is the captive's insurance liabilities multiplied by the Schedule 3 rate. Unlike the fee for other authorised insurers, the Schedule 2 calculation is not capped at HK$7 million. Schedule 3 sets the rate at 0.0039 percent from 26 June 2022 and for each subsequent year beginning on 26 June. The Authority's consultation paper of 4 November 2026 still describes the current variable rate as 0.0039 percent. Its proposed increase is for long-term business and is not in force. The Schedule 2 fee is payable upon authorisation. Company-registry incorporation fees are not set in Schedule 2.
Annual Maintenance Fee
The annual fee under section 13(1)(b) is the same aggregate for a captive insurer: a fixed fee of HK$30,000 plus the variable fee at 0.0039 percent of insurance liabilities, without the HK$7 million cap that applies to Schedule 1. The 4 November 2026 consultation says a special purpose insurer does not pay the variable component. The definition of insurance liabilities used after the 1 July 2024 risk-based capital commencement was not re-extracted from a later amending instrument.
Annual Filing Requirements
The Authority's regulatory-returns page, in the captive-insurer section current as of the March 2026 and April 2026 forms, requires a quarterly return of financial position, capital adequacy, and business information within eight weeks after each quarter-end, and an annual return of financial position, capital adequacy, business, governance, and supplementary information within six months after the financial year-end. Captive insurers are not given the monthly return required of other authorised insurers on that page. The captive requirements page says a captive notifies the Authority after appointing a key person in a control function, rather than obtaining prior approval, and is exempt from appointing a certifying actuary under rule 3 of Cap. 41Q. It is exempt from the assets-in-Hong Kong requirement that applies to a non-Hong Kong general insurer's onshore business, from Guideline GL20 on cybersecurity, and from Guideline GL21 on enterprise risk management. Guideline GL10 on corporate governance is encouraged rather than required. The risk-based capital overview says an insurer generally submits an own-risk-and-solvency assessment at least annually under GL21. The captive page's exemption from GL21 is the captive-specific statement. Insurance (Public Disclosure) Rules (Cap. 41W) came into operation on 22 May 2026. The legislative-developments page does not state a captive exemption from those rules.
