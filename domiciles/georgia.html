Georgia

Georgia licenses captive insurance companies under the Georgia Captive Insurance Company Act, Chapter 41 of Title 33, enacted in 1988 and administered by the Captives Division of the Office of the Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire. The chapter covers pure, association, agency, industrial insured, and sponsored captives, and risk retention group captives. A sponsored captive may use incorporated or unincorporated protected cells. House Bill 348, effective 1 June 2025, amended the permissible-business section so that a captive may engage in any insurance or reinsurance the Commissioner deems reasonable, still subject to the ownership and risk limits in O.C.G.A. section 33-41-3. Workers' compensation and motor-vehicle policies must meet Georgia's minimum coverage requirements. Surplus floors are $250,000 for a pure, agency, or sponsored captive and $500,000 for an association captive, an industrial insured captive, or a risk retention group. A dormant captive maintains at least $25,000. Direct premium tax is 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent thereafter. Assumed-reinsurance premium tax runs from 0.225 percent down to 0.025 percent across $20 million bands. The annual maximum is $100,000. The statute does not state a minimum premium tax. The license fee is $600 at application and the renewal fee is $500 each later year. The Division's formation page says proposed articles are approved or disapproved within 45 days and the certificate of authority within 90 days, with a further 90-day extension available under O.C.G.A. section 33-3-15. The Captives Division contact page publishes captivedivision@oci.ga.gov and does not name a captive program head or a section headcount. The Division pages reviewed do not publish a current captive census.

Tax Environment
O.C.G.A. section 33-41-22 imposes the captive tax in lieu of any other taxes imposed by Title 33. Direct premiums, after return premiums and policyholder dividends or premium deposits returned or credited, are taxed at 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent on each dollar thereafter. A risk retention group captive is subject to that direct-premium tax only on coverages within Georgia. Assumed-reinsurance premium is taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, 0.050 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.025 percent thereafter. Reinsurance tax does not apply to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of loss reserves and other liabilities of another insurer under common ownership and control that the Commissioner verifies is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer. If the calculated tax exceeds $100,000 in a year, the tax is $100,000. The section does not state a minimum tax. Two or more captives under common ownership and control, other than sponsored captives, are taxed as one captive. Common ownership and control means more than 50 percent of the voting securities or membership interests. A protected cell is a captive for this tax. The aggregation rule applies to each cell and its participants, and the sponsored captive collects and remits the cells' tax. The annual return and payment are due 1 March under Rule 120-2-45-.13. A late return or payment draws a $5,000 penalty. An extension of not more than 30 days may be granted, with interest of 1 percent per month. Rule 120-2-45-.13(3) says a risk retention group files and pays premium tax under O.C.G.A. section 33-40-5. A separate section 33-40-5 rate is not restated here. A dormant captive that holds a certificate of dormancy is not liable for the section 33-41-22 tax. No Georgia corporate income-tax rate for captives is stated in Chapter 41.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive At least $250,000 in surplus. The Commissioner sets the amount individually and may require more based on the business plan (O.C.G.A. section 33-41-8(a)(1)).
Association captive At least $500,000 in surplus (section 33-41-8(a)(2)).
Agency captive At least $250,000 in surplus (section 33-41-8(a)(3)).
Industrial insured captive At least $500,000 in surplus (section 33-41-8(a)(4)).
Risk retention group At least $500,000 in surplus (section 33-41-8(a)(5)).
Sponsored captive At least $250,000 in surplus under section 33-41-8(a)(6), and at least $250,000 in capital and surplus under section 33-41-101(9)(B), available to pay expenses and claims of the sponsored captive. Article 2 does not state a separate dollar floor for an unincorporated protected cell.
Dormant captive At least $25,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus while a certificate of dormancy is in force. If the Commissioner allows dormancy while liabilities remain, the Commissioner may require more capital, surplus, and reserves (section 33-41-23).
Form and deposit Minimum capital or surplus of up to $500,000 is held in cash, FDIC-insured certificates of deposit, insured savings accounts or certificates, or letters of credit that meet section 33-41-9. Additional capital above $500,000 may be held in eligible investments under section 33-11-5, shareholder obligations secured by letters of credit, or other investments the Commissioner approves. An association captive, an industrial insured captive, and a risk retention group must deposit with the state before the certificate of authority is issued. The current text of section 33-41-8(c) requires that deposit and does not state its dollar amount.

Audits are Rule 120-2-45-.09. Examinations and the organizational-examination fee are Rule 120-2-45-.08. Premium-tax filing is Rule 120-2-45-.13.

Licensing Speed
The Division's formation page states that proposed articles of incorporation are approved or disapproved within 45 days, and that the certificate of authority is then approved or disapproved within 90 days. The Division may extend that certificate review by another 90 days under O.C.G.A. section 33-3-15 by written notice. A redomestication application is approved or disapproved within 45 days under Rule 120-2-45-.17. O.C.G.A. section 33-41-10(d) says that, once the Commissioner is satisfied the application complies, the certificate is issued promptly and authorizes business until 30 June.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent thereafter. A risk retention group is taxed under section 33-41-22 only on direct premiums for Georgia coverages, and Rule 120-2-45-.13(3) points risk-retention-group premium-tax filing to O.C.G.A. section 33-40-5. Assumed reinsurance: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $20 million bands. No minimum is stated in section 33-41-22. Annual maximum $100,000. Due 1 March. Late filing or payment penalty $5,000.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Rule 120-2-45-.12 requires a $600 license fee when the application for a certificate of authority is filed. If the Commissioner requires an organizational examination, Rule 120-2-45-.08 requires $3,000 payable to the appointed examiner, and the Commissioner may require a higher amount in writing. O.C.G.A. section 33-41-10(c) also requires the applicant to pay the costs of examining, investigating, and processing the original or renewal application. Corporate formation fees payable to the Georgia Secretary of State are not set in Chapter 41.
Annual Maintenance Fee
A renewal fee of $500 is due for each year after the initial year of licensure (Rule 120-2-45-.12). A certificate of dormancy is renewed every five years for $500 (O.C.G.A. section 33-41-23(b)(3)).
Annual Filing Requirements
Each captive files the reports of its business affairs required by O.C.G.A. section 33-3-21, as section 33-41-15 provides. Rule 120-2-45-.07 requires, on or before 1 March, a report of financial condition as of the preceding 31 December, verified by the oath of an executive officer. A pure captive elects generally accepted accounting principles or statutory accounting principles. An industrial insured captive and an association captive use statutory accounting principles. An industrial insured captive, an association captive, and a risk retention group file the NAIC property-and-casualty blank by 1 March. An industrial insured or association captive that directly writes workers' compensation, and every risk retention group, also files quarterly NAIC blanks as of 31 March, 30 June, and 30 September, due 15 May, 15 August, and 15 November. Rule 120-2-45-.09 requires an audited financial report by 30 June. The audit includes an opinion on the adequacy of loss and loss-expense reserves by a Fellow of the Casualty Actuarial Society, a member of the American Academy of Actuaries, or another person who has demonstrated competence in loss-reserve evaluation and whom the Commissioner approves. The premium-tax return is due 1 March. A sponsored captive's annual financial report includes the financial experience of each protected cell. The Commissioner may examine a captive at any time, and Rule 120-2-45-.08 says an examination occurs no less often than once every five years unless the Commissioner makes a written finding that a longer period is warranted. At least one director or manager is a Georgia resident, and the company reports a director or manager change within 30 days. A material change that is not disapproved within 30 days after submission is deemed approved. A dormant captive files a financial-condition report by 1 March and does not file the other annual forms, fees, or documents that Chapter 41 would otherwise require. Filings and payments are submitted through the GIMS portal.
