Florida

Florida licenses captive insurers under Part V of Chapter 628, Florida Statutes. The part originated in chapter 82-243, Laws of Florida (1982). Chapter 2012-151 specified formation, capital, reporting, and reinsurance in more detail, and chapter 2026-72, approved on 11 May 2026 and effective 1 July 2026, added protected cell captive insurance companies. The Office of Insurance Regulation licenses pure, industrial insured, special purpose, and protected cell captives, and separately licenses captive reinsurance companies. A pure captive insures its parent, affiliated companies, and controlled unaffiliated businesses. An industrial insured captive insures the industrial insureds that own it, and their affiliates, and may reinsure insurers only on those risks. A special purpose captive does not meet another captive definition and may insure only its parent. A protected cell captive insures participants through participant contracts and segregates each cell from the others and from the general account. A captive reinsurance company is a stock corporation, wholly owned by a qualifying reinsurer parent, and may not insure risks directly. Unimpaired paid-in capital is at least $100,000 for a pure or protected cell stock captive and $200,000 for an industrial insured stock captive. A nonprofit pure captive maintains unrestricted net assets of at least $250,000, and a nonprofit protected cell captive maintains at least $100,000. Unimpaired surplus is at least $150,000 for a pure captive, $100,000 for a protected cell captive, $300,000 for an industrial insured stock captive, and $500,000 for an industrial insured mutual captive. Special purpose amounts are set by the Office. A captive reinsurer maintains capital or surplus of at least the greater of $300 million or 10 percent of reserves. Section 624.509 taxes direct premiums on Florida risks at 1.75 percent and omits premiums on reinsurance accepted. A salary credit and a credit for Florida corporate income tax paid are together limited to 65 percent of the premium tax after the deductions named in the Department of Revenue instructions. The Office's 2026 captive application states that $1,525 is payable when the application is filed, under sections 624.501(17) and 628.905(4), together with the $1,000 annual license tax under section 624.501(3). The Office does not publish a captive census or a captive-unit headcount. Its captive overview page still describes three captive-insurance types and does not describe the protected cell captives added on 1 July 2026.

Tax Environment
Section 628.909 applies Chapter 624 to captive insurance companies and to industrial insured captives, except listed sections that do not include section 624.509. Section 624.509(1) requires each insurer, on or before 1 March, to pay the Department of Revenue a tax on premiums, assessments, membership fees, policy fees, and gross deposits received during the preceding calendar year. The rate in section 624.509(1)(a) is 1.75 percent of the gross amount of receipts on life and health policies covering Florida residents and on other policies and contracts covering property, subjects, or risks located, resident, or to be performed in Florida. Annuity receipts are taxed at 1 percent under paragraph (b), and direct written bail-bond premiums are taxed at 1.75 percent under paragraph (c). The paragraph (a) base omits premiums on reinsurance accepted and subtracts return premiums or assessments. It does not allow a deduction for reinsurance ceded, cash surrender values, prompt-payment discounts or refunds, or dividends credited to policyholders. Rule 12B-8.001 expressly applies that 1.75 percent rate to insurance issued by a captive or industrial captive insurer under Part V of Chapter 628. Section 624.509(5) allows a credit equal to 15 percent of salaries paid to employees located or based in Florida and covered by Chapter 443. An affiliated group that includes at least one insurer writing Florida premiums may instead use the alternative salary credit, if it elected that method on or before 1 August 2005. Section 624.509(4) credits Florida corporate income tax paid against the premium tax. The Department of Revenue's 2026 Form DR-908 instructions state that the corporate-income-tax credit and the salary credit together may not exceed 65 percent of the premium tax due after deductions for the excise taxes paid to the Firefighters' and Municipal Police Officers' Retirement Trust Funds and for the workers' compensation assessment. Part V does not state a captive-specific corporate income-tax rate or an in-lieu exemption from it. The Florida corporate income-tax rate was not taken from Chapter 220 in this review. A protected cell's premium tax follows the cell on a conversion or disaffiliation: the prior entity is responsible for premium tax on transactions before the final date, and the successor is responsible on and after that date (section 628.921(9)(e), chapter 2026-72).

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure captive, stock At least $100,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital (section 628.907(1)(b)) and at least $150,000 of unimpaired surplus (section 628.908(1)(a)).
Pure captive, nonprofit At least $250,000 of unrestricted net assets (section 628.907(2)(b)). Section 628.908(1)(a) also requires at least $150,000 of unimpaired surplus for a pure captive and does not exclude a nonprofit pure captive. Contributions to a nonprofit captive must be cash, a cash equivalent, or an irrevocable letter of credit issued by a Florida-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank with a Florida branch, or as approved by the Office (section 628.907(3)).
Industrial insured captive, stock At least $200,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital (section 628.907(1)(c)) and at least $300,000 of unimpaired surplus (section 628.908(1)(c)).
Industrial insured captive, mutual At least $500,000 of unimpaired surplus (section 628.908(1)(d)). Section 628.907 does not set a separate paid-in capital amount for a mutual industrial insured captive. It may be incorporated as a mutual insurer without capital stock (section 628.910(2)(b)).
Protected cell captive At least $100,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital if incorporated with capital stock, or at least $100,000 of unrestricted net assets if incorporated as a nonprofit (section 628.907(1)(a) and (2)(a)), and at least $100,000 of unimpaired surplus (section 628.908(1)(b)). Section 628.921, effective 1 July 2026, does not set a separate dollar floor for an individual protected cell. When a cell is established, assets attributed to it must at least equal the reserves and other insurance liabilities attributed to that cell. The company's capital and surplus must at all times be available to pay expenses of or claims against the company.
Special purpose captive The Office sets the unimpaired paid-in capital, the unrestricted net assets of a nonprofit, and the unimpaired surplus after considering the business plan, feasibility study, pro forma financial statements and projections, and the risks to be insured (sections 628.907(1)(d), 628.907(2)(c), and 628.908(1)(e)).
Captive reinsurance company Capital or unimpaired surplus of at least the greater of $300 million or 10 percent of reserves. The test is capital or surplus, not both. Surplus may be cash or securities permitted by Part II of Chapter 625. The Office may prescribe additional capital or surplus (section 628.914).
Additional capital and conditional license The Office may prescribe additional capital or net assets based on the type, volume, and nature of the business. Those additional amounts must be cash, a cash equivalent, an irrevocable letter of credit from a Florida-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank with a Florida branch, or as approved by the Office, or securities invested under Part II of Chapter 625 (section 628.907(5)). The Office may issue a license conditioned on evidence of the minimum capital or surplus. Until that evidence is provided, the captive may not issue a policy, assume a liability, or otherwise provide coverage. The Office may revoke the conditional license if the evidence is not provided within the period it sets, which may not exceed one year (sections 628.907(4) and 628.908(2)).

Regulatory Framework

Chapter 628, Part V, Florida Statutes, sections 628.901 to 628.921, as amended by chapter 2026-72, Laws of Florida

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Part V originated in chapter 82-243, Laws of Florida. Chapter 2012-151 (House Bill 1101) amended the formation, capital, reporting, and reinsurance provisions. Chapter 2013-209 also amended the part. Chapter 2026-72, Committee Substitute for Committee Substitute for House Bill 883, was approved by the Governor on 11 May 2026, filed with the Secretary of State the same day, and took effect on 1 July 2026. It added protected cell definitions and section 628.921. Capital and net assets are section 628.907. Surplus is section 628.908. Captive reinsurance capital is section 628.914. Licensing and the captive fee are section 628.905. Reports are section 628.911. Which other Insurance Code provisions apply is section 628.909. Incorporation is section 628.910. The general fee schedule is section 624.501. Premium tax is section 624.509, and Florida Administrative Code Rule 12B-8.001 lists captives in the 1.75 percent computation. The Department of Revenue's 2026 Form DR-908 instructions state the 65 percent combined credit limit under sections 624.509(4) and 624.509(5).

Licensing Speed
Section 628.905 does not set a number of days for a decision. If the Office is satisfied that the filing complies, it may grant a license until the next 1 March, when the license may be renewed. The Company Admissions page states that, after an application is accepted and considered complete, the Office strives to conclude the review within 60 days. Sections 628.907(4) and 628.908(2) separately allow a conditional license, for no more than one year, while the captive produces evidence of the minimum capital or surplus. The captive may not provide coverage during that period.
Premium Tax Rate
1.75 percent of the section 624.509(1)(a) gross receipts on Florida risks, due 1 March. Premiums on reinsurance accepted are omitted. Return premiums are deducted. Reinsurance ceded, surrender values, prompt-payment discounts, and policyholder dividends are not deducted. Annuities are taxed at 1 percent. The 15 percent Florida-salary credit and the credit for Florida corporate income tax paid together may not exceed 65 percent of the premium tax after the firefighter, municipal police, and workers' compensation assessment deductions stated in the 2026 Form DR-908 instructions.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 628.905(4) requires a nonrefundable fee of $1,500 to process the license application of a captive insurance company or a captive reinsurance company. Section 624.501(17) charges $25 to file an application for a permit to form an insurer under Chapter 628. The Office's captive application package, in the 2026 company-admissions forms, states that the applicant pays $1,525 under those two sections when the application is filed. The same package also requires the $1,000 annual license tax under section 624.501(3) at filing. Both amounts are nonrefundable. Section 628.905(4)(b) allows a $5 fee for a document that needs a certification of authenticity or an Office signature. Fingerprint processing is charged at cost under section 624.501(23). The amount is not fixed in that subsection. Organization fees payable to the Department of State are those required by the Florida Business Corporation Act or the Florida Nonprofit Corporation Act. Part V does not set the dollar amount. The captive overview page states an initial license application fee of $1,500 and does not mention the $25 permit fee.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Section 628.905(4)(a) requires an annual renewal fee of $1,000. Section 624.501(3) imposes an annual license tax of $1,000 on each domestic, foreign, and alien insurer, and Chapter 624 applies to these captives. The Office's 2026 application describes the $1,000 due at filing as the section 624.501(3) annual license tax. The captive overview page describes a $1,000 annual renewal fee. The pages reviewed do not say whether those two statutory $1,000 amounts are collected once or twice. A license authorizes business until the next 1 March and may then be renewed (section 628.905(5)). Section 624.501(4) sets a $250 fee for an insurer's annual statement and $250 for a quarterly statement. Section 628.911(1) says a captive may not be required to make any annual report except as Part V provides, so those statement fees are not restated here as captive fees.
Annual Filing Requirements
Section 628.911(1) says a captive insurance company may not be required to make any annual report except as Part V provides. On or before 1 March, a captive insurance company or a captive reinsurance company submits a report of its financial condition, verified by the oath of two executive officers. The report uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the Office approves statutory accounting principles, with the modifications the Office requires or accepts, plus any additional information the Office requires. The Financial Services Commission may adopt the report form by rule. A captive may apply to report on a fiscal year-end consistent with its parent's fiscal year. If that date is granted, the report is due 60 days after the fiscal year-end. A protected cell captive must file the financial reports the Office requires, and each report must include accounting statements for the financial experience of each protected cell. It must notify the Office in writing within 10 business days of any protected cell that is insolvent or unable to meet its claims or expenses. The Office's captive page states that a captive reinsurance company must file an annual actuarial opinion on loss and loss-adjustment-expense reserves from an independent actuary who is not an employee of the company or its affiliates, and that loss reserves may be discounted at treasury rates under section 628.912. A captive must hold at least one board meeting each year in Florida, keep its principal place of business in Florida, and appoint a resident registered agent (section 628.905(2)). A captive incorporated in Florida as a corporation or nonprofit must have at least one Florida-resident director (section 628.910(7)). It may not have fewer than three incorporators, of whom at least two are Florida residents (section 628.910(3)). Chapter 624 applies except the sections excluded by section 628.909, so the Office's Chapter 624 examination authority applies. Part V does not state a separate examination interval.
