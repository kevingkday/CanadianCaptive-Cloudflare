Arizona

Arizona's Captive Insurance Division, in the Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions, licenses captives under Arizona Revised Statutes sections 20-1098 through 20-1098.24. The division states that the captive statute took effect in 2002. A captive may be pure, group, agency, or protected cell, and may be formed as a stock insurer, limited liability company, mutual, reciprocal, or nonprofit. A group captive is an association captive, an industry group captive, or a risk retention group. The statute also provides for branch captives and for dormant captives. Minimum unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus is $250,000 for a pure or protected cell captive and $500,000 for a group, agency, or reciprocal captive. A pure or group captive that transacts only reinsurance holds half of the applicable amount. A dormant captive with a certificate of dormancy holds $125,000. Fees under section 20-167(F) are in lieu of premium tax and other state and local taxes, except taxes on real and tangible personal property located in Arizona, transaction privilege and use taxes, and the section 20-224 premium tax that an agency captive owes on specified life and disability policies to the extent it exceeds those fees. As of 30 June 2026 the division counted 209 captives: 181 pure including reinsurers, 6 protected cell, 5 industry group, 1 association, 4 agency, 12 risk retention groups, and no branch captives. Its growth table lists 14 licenses issued in 2026 beside that cumulative count of 209. The director must approve or deny an application within 30 days after deeming it complete. The governing body meets at least once a year in Arizona, and the captive keeps its principal place of business, books and records, and an Arizona manager in the state.

Tax Environment
A.R.S. section 20-1098.17 provides that the captive fees paid under section 20-167(F) are payment in full and in lieu of all other state, county, district, municipal, and school taxes, licenses, and excises, except a tax on real and tangible personal property located in Arizona, the transaction privilege tax and use tax under title 42, chapter 5, articles 1 and 4, and transaction privilege and use taxes imposed by a county, city, or town. An agency captive insurer that insures the life or disability risks specified in section 20-1098.01(A)(3)(b) pays the premium tax prescribed in section 20-224 on those policies to the extent it exceeds the fees paid under section 20-167. The Captive Insurance Division states that Arizona has no insurance premium tax. The annual regulatory charge published by the division is the flat license renewal fee, not a premium-tax rate.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure At least $250,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus in combination (A.R.S. section 20-1098.03(A)(1)). The division publishes the same floor.
Group At least $500,000 (section 20-1098.03(A)(2)). A group captive insurer is an association captive, an industry group captive, or a risk retention group (section 20-1098(16)). The division lists association and industry group captives, and risk retention groups, at $500,000.
Agency At least $500,000 (section 20-1098.03(A)(3)).
Protected cell At least $250,000 (section 20-1098.03(A)(4)). The division publishes the same floor.
Reciprocal At least $500,000 for a captive insurer organized as a reciprocal insurer (section 20-1098.03(A)(5)).
Pure or group, reinsurance only One-half of the applicable pure or group amount (section 20-1098.03(A)(6)). The division states that pure reinsurers hold 50 percent of the indicated amounts.
Dormant A dormant captive insurer that has been issued a certificate of dormancy maintains unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus of at least $125,000 (section 20-1098.24(B)(1)). A risk retention group is excluded from the dormant-captive definition.
Branch The division lists branch captive insurers as a type and counted none at 30 June 2026. Section 20-1098.03 does not state a separate dollar floor for a branch captive.
Form and additional capital The statutory minimum is cash or an irrevocable and unconditional letter of credit with an evergreen clause, payable to, filed with, and held by the director (section 20-1098.03(B)). The director may prescribe additional capital and surplus based on the type, volume, and nature of insurance (section 20-1098.03(C)).

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Arizona Revised Statutes sections 20-1098 through 20-1098.24. Definitions are section 20-1098. Licensing, permitted business, the in-state meeting and principal-place requirements, and the 30-day decision are section 20-1098.01. Minimum capital and surplus are section 20-1098.03. Protected cells are section 20-1098.05. The annual report and audit are section 20-1098.07. The fee-in-lieu and premium-tax rule is section 20-1098.17. Dormancy is section 20-1098.24. Certificate of authority fees for captive insurers are set by the director under section 20-167(F). The Captive Insurance Division states that the captive statute's effective date is 2002. A session-law citation for the original enactment was not retrieved, so 2002 is the effective year the division publishes and is not recorded here as a separately verified enactment year.

Licensing Speed
The director shall approve or deny an application within 30 days after the director deems the application complete (A.R.S. section 20-1098.01(L)).
Premium Tax Rate
No generally applicable captive premium tax. Fees under section 20-167(F) are in lieu of other state and local taxes, subject to the property-tax, transaction-privilege-tax, use-tax, and agency-captive exceptions in section 20-1098.17. The standing annual regulatory charge is the $5,500 license renewal, plus $2,500 for each protected cell.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
DIFI's captive payment page lists an original insurer license application total of $1,175 and an original per-cell application of $1,000. The division's fee table lists a $100 examiners' revolving-fund deposit, a $75 charter-document filing fee, and a $60 articles of incorporation or organization fee payable to the Arizona Corporation Commission. The payment page also lists a $200 license-amendment fee and a $30 amended-charter fee. Fees are nonrefundable. The applicant may also be billed for outside specialists used in the review. Section 20-1098.01(J) requires the nonrefundable issuance fee prescribed under section 20-167.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Captive insurer annual license renewal of $5,500, and a protected-cell annual renewal of $2,500, as published by the division and on its payment page. The renewal fee is paid not earlier than 1 July and not later than 1 September (section 20-1098.01(J)). A certificate of dormancy is renewed every five years; the statute points that fee to section 20-167 and the division page does not publish a separate dormancy amount.
Annual Filing Requirements
Not later than 90 days after the end of its fiscal year, a captive insurer submits a report of its financial condition verified by the oath of two executive officers. The report may use generally accepted accounting principles unless the director requires statutory accounting principles, with any modifications the director requires (A.R.S. section 20-1098.07(A)). Financial statements are audited by an independent certified public accountant unless the director determines that an audit is not necessary, and the audit opinion is filed not later than six months after fiscal year-end. Unless the director grants an exemption, the annual report meets the applicable life and health, property and casualty, or NAIC health actuarial requirements. A protected cell captive also files an accounting of each protected cell with the annual report. A branch captive files, within 60 days after its fiscal year-end, the reports required by its home jurisdiction. A dormant captive with a certificate of dormancy files a verified financial-condition report within 90 days after fiscal year-end. The license renewal fee is due between 1 July and 1 September. The division states that an exemption from the annual actuarial certification and audit may apply to small captive insurers, and that there is no routine statutory examination for pure captive insurers.
