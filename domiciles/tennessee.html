Tennessee

Tennessee regulates captives through the Department of Commerce and Insurance Captive Insurance Section under the Revised Tennessee Captive Insurance Act, Tennessee Code Title 56, Chapter 13. The department states that captive legislation was first enacted in 1978 and was thoroughly revised in 2011. The Act covers pure, association, industrial insured, agency, risk retention group, protected cell, incorporated cell, branch, and special purpose financial captives. An incorporated protected cell may be an individual series of a limited liability company, and a series cell formed after 1 July 2015 uses a series-cell name. Unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus is at least $250,000 for a pure or agency captive, $500,000 for an association or industrial insured captive, $1,000,000 for a risk retention group, and $100,000 for a protected cell captive. A special purpose financial captive starts with at least $250,000 in cash. A dormant captive holding a letter of dormancy maintains $25,000 and is not liable for the minimum premium tax. Direct premium tax is 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent after that. Assumed-reinsurance tax is 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $20 million bands. The ordinary minimum is $5,000 and the ordinary maximum is $100,000. A protected cell captive with more than 10 cells has a $10,000 minimum and a maximum of $100,000 plus $5,000 for each cell over 10. An alien company redomesticating to Tennessee may elect to forgo premium tax in either its first or its second year, but not both, and a clawback applies if it leaves within five years. The Captive Statistics page lists 194 captive insurance companies: 111 pure, 79 protected cell companies, and 4 association. The newest license date on that list is 1 October 2026. The page does not state a total, a cell count, or whether every listed company is active. On 17 March 2026 the department reported five new captives and 50 new cells licensed in 2025, and 184 captives and 703 cells at that date. The Spring 2026 newsletter reported 184 captives and 705 cells. A complete application is processed in approximately 30 days.

Tax Environment
Under Tenn. Code Ann. section 56-13-114, each captive pays the department by 15 March. Direct premiums, after return premiums, are taxed at 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent on each dollar after that. Annuity considerations are not taxed. Assumed reinsurance premium is taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $20 million, 0.150 percent on the next $20 million, 0.050 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.025 percent after that. No reinsurance tax applies to risks taxed on a direct basis, or to a qualifying assumption of reserves and liabilities of an insurer under common ownership and control that the commissioner verifies is part of a plan to discontinue that insurer's operations. Except for a protected cell captive with more than 10 cells, the annual minimum aggregate tax is $5,000 and the annual maximum aggregate tax is $100,000. For a protected cell captive with more than 10 cells, the minimum is $10,000 and the maximum is $100,000 plus $5,000 multiplied by the number of cells over 10. A dormant captive with a letter of dormancy is not liable for the minimum. The section states that this tax is all the tax collectible under Tennessee law from the captive and from an insured on payments to the captive, except ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used in the production of income. A company redomesticating under section 56-13-123 is taxed only on premiums paid after redomestication. One that redomesticates after 1 July pays half the minimum tax in its first year. An alien company may elect to forgo premium tax in either its first or its second year after redomestication, but not both. If it then surrenders its license or redomesticates away within five years, it immediately pays the foregone tax plus 10 percent per year from the original due date.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Pure Not less than $250,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus (Tenn. Code Ann. section 56-13-105(a)(1)).
Agency Not less than $250,000 (section 56-13-105(a)(6)). An agency captive is owned by an insurance agency or brokerage and only reinsures risks of policies placed by or through that agency or brokerage.
Association Not less than $500,000 (section 56-13-105(a)(2)).
Industrial insured Not less than $500,000 (section 56-13-105(a)(3)).
Risk retention group Not less than $1,000,000 (section 56-13-105(a)(4)).
Protected cell Not less than $100,000 for the protected cell captive insurance company (section 56-13-105(a)(5)). A separate statutory dollar floor for each protected cell was not stated in section 56-13-105.
Special purpose financial captive Initial minimum capitalization of not less than $250,000, all of it in cash (section 56-13-406(a)). The commissioner may require additional capitalization after reviewing the plan of operation, feasibility study, pro formas, and the risks.
Dormant A dormant captive that has been issued a letter of dormancy maintains unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus of not less than $25,000 (section 56-13-124(c)(1)).
Additional capital and form The commissioner may prescribe additional capital and surplus based on the type, volume, and nature of the business (section 56-13-105(b)). Capital required by subsection (a) must be cash, a cash equivalent, marketable securities meeting the statute's rating limits, or an irrevocable letter of credit from a bank the commissioner approves. The department's licensing page requires the minimum to be provided on its irrevocable letter of credit or securities account control agreement, and it does not accept changes to those forms.

Regulatory Framework

Revised Tennessee Captive Insurance Act, Tennessee Code Annotated Title 56, Chapter 13

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Tennessee Code Annotated Title 56, Chapter 13, the Revised Tennessee Captive Insurance Act. The Captive Insurance Section states that captive legislation was first enacted in 1978 and thoroughly revised by the General Assembly in 2011 (2011 Public Chapter 468). Part 1 is general provisions, including capital in section 56-13-105, annual reports in section 56-13-108, examinations in section 56-13-109, premium tax in section 56-13-114, redomestication in section 56-13-123, and dormancy in section 56-13-124. Part 2 is protected cell captive insurance companies, including series cells in section 56-13-204. Part 3 is branch captive insurance companies. Part 4 is special purpose financial captives, including minimum capitalization in section 56-13-406. Section histories through the 2025 code include 2024 Public Chapter 643.

Licensing Speed
Approximately 30 days from the time the Captive Insurance Section receives a complete application package.
Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.4 percent on the first $20 million and 0.3 percent thereafter. Assumed reinsurance premiums: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, 0.050 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $20 million bands. Ordinary minimum $5,000 and ordinary maximum $100,000. A protected cell captive with more than 10 cells has a $10,000 minimum and a maximum of $100,000 plus $5,000 per cell over 10. A dormant captive with a letter of dormancy is not liable for the minimum. Due 15 March.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
The Captive Insurance Section lists an application fee of $675, due before approval of the captive or cell, and a certificate of authority issuance fee of $440. A change in the business plan of operations is $400. A certificate of compliance is $7. If the commissioner assigns the application to an outside actuarial firm, the applicant pays that review. Corporate formation fees are paid to the Tennessee Secretary of State and are not set on the captive fee list.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual statement filing fee of $515, listed by the Captive Insurance Section. No separate annual license-renewal fee was published on that list.
Annual Filing Requirements
Before 15 March, each captive submits a report of its financial condition, verified by the oath of two executive officers, on the Captive Annual Statement Form. A risk retention group submits that report before 1 March. Reporting uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the commissioner requires, approves, or accepts statutory accounting principles or another comprehensive basis (Tenn. Code Ann. section 56-13-108). A captive other than a risk retention group may be allowed to file 75 days after its fiscal year-end, and a pure or industrial insured captive on a fiscal year still files the premium-tax support before 15 March unless the commissioner approves another tax date. The department requires loss and loss-expense reserves to be certified by a Fellow of the Casualty Actuarial Society, a member in good standing of the American Academy of Actuaries, or another person who has demonstrated competence in loss-reserve evaluation and whom the commissioner has approved. Captives in good standing that have an annual audit by an approved CPA are placed on a five-year examination cycle. Captives that do not have an annual audit are placed on a maximum three-year examination cycle. A dormant captive with a letter of dormancy still files the section 56-13-108 report before 15 March. Filings can be submitted through the department's CORE system.
