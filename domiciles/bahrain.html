Bahrain

Bahrain licenses captive insurers through the Central Bank of Bahrain under Volume 3 of the CBB Rulebook. The Bank's insurance page states that the CBB has been the insurance regulator since 2002. The capital-adequacy module is a directive issued under Article 38 of the Central Bank of Bahrain and Financial Institutions Law 2006. A captive is Category C1 or Category C2. Category C1 may insure only non-liability risks of its shareholders or of subsidiary or associated companies of those shareholders. Category C2 is limited to the same related risks and may include liability risks if the CBB is satisfied that the activity, capital structure and management protect potential third-party claimants. Captives are not subject to the BD 5 million Tier 1 floor for a Bahraini insurance firm, or the BD 10 million floor where that firm's business is limited to reinsurance. They must keep capital available above the greater of the required solvency margin and a minimum fund of BD 75,000 for Category C1 or BD 300,000 for Category C2. A captive may be a Bahraini special purpose vehicle or one of the other insurance-firm legal forms. The annual licensing fee is a fixed BD 1,000. Central Bank of Bahrain correspondence, November 2026, states that the captive licence application fee is BD 100, non-refundable, and payable on submission through the CBB online application portal (https://www.cbb.gov.bh/application-for-a-license/). After receipt of the required forms and supporting documents, the Bank assesses the application and the proposed business model before determining next steps. That correspondence does not state a processing timeline. The fact sheet counts 1 captive among 21 locally incorporated insurance and reinsurance firms. That sheet dates the wider count of insurance companies and organisations at December 2025 and does not date the captive line separately, or publish new licences, surrenders, or a staff count. Central Bank of Bahrain correspondence, November 2026, points to the CBB Licensing Directory for institutions currently licensed and does not state a captive count, the date of that count, or figures for new licences and surrenders. The solvency-margin percentages in CA-2.1.10 to CA-2.1.12 remain unverified. Licensing enquiries go to licensing@cbb.gov.bh.

Tax Environment
The CBB Rulebook fee for a captive is a fixed annual licensing fee, not a premium tax. The National Bureau for Revenue states that value-added tax has been charged at the standard rate of 10 percent since 1 January 2022. Its financial-services guidance states that life insurance contracts, life reinsurance contracts and transfers of those contracts are exempt, and that other insurance services, such as medical and motor insurance, are taxable at 10 percent, with travel insurance zero-rated in some cases. Decree-Law No. 11 of 2024 imposes a domestic minimum top-up tax of 15 percent, from 1 January 2025, on multinational enterprises whose annual global revenue is at least EUR 750 million. On 30 December 2025 the Bureau described a draft law, to be referred to the legislature, that would charge 10 percent on profits above BD 200,000 of local companies whose revenue exceeds BD 1 million or whose annual net profit exceeds BD 200,000, with the stated aim of applying it in 2027. The Bureau said a group already within the 15 percent top-up tax would not also pay that 10 percent. The draft is not stated as a tax in force. No captive-specific income-tax exemption was stated on the Bureau pages or in the rulebook modules reviewed.

Financial Requirements

Minimum Capital & Surplus

Structure Type Minimum Capital
Category C1 Minimum fund of BD 75,000 (CA-2.1.5(e)). A Category C1 insurer may insure only the insurance risks, other than liability risk, of its shareholders or of subsidiary or associated companies of its shareholders (CA-2.1.6(e)).
Category C2 Minimum fund of BD 300,000 (CA-2.1.5(f)). A Category C2 insurer is restricted to the risks of its shareholders or of their subsidiary or associated companies, and its business may include liability risks if the CBB is satisfied that the activity, capital structure and management provide sufficient protection to potential third-party claimants (CA-2.1.6(f)).
Tier 1 capital Captive insurers are not subject to a minimum Tier 1 capital. A Bahraini insurance firm otherwise maintains minimum Tier 1 capital of BD 5 million, or BD 10 million where its business is limited to reinsurance (CA-1.2.1).
Capital available and solvency margin Every insurance firm must at all times maintain capital available in excess of the greater of the required solvency margin and the minimum fund (CA-1.2.1). The required solvency margin for long-term insurance business is determined under CA-2.1.9, and for general insurance business under CA-2.1.12 (CA-2.1.7). Where the long-term aggregate falls below the minimum fund, the minimum fund is substituted. The percentage components of CA-2.1.10, CA-2.1.11 and CA-2.1.12 were not extracted from the rulebook section retrieved, so they are not restated.
Legal form and incorporation capital A captive's legal status must be a Bahraini special purpose vehicle established to carry out the captive's activities, or a Bahraini joint stock company, a Bahraini branch of an overseas insurance or reinsurance firm, or a Bahraini exempt company that was incorporated and licensed to conduct insurance business before 1 January 2005 (AU-2.1.1 and AU-2.1.2). A Bahraini special purpose vehicle is a closed joint stock company. The rulebook states that the minimum capital required by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry to incorporate that company is BD 1,000, and that the captive must still satisfy the Module CA capital requirements (AU-2.1.3).

Regulatory Framework

Central Bank of Bahrain Rulebook, Volume 3 (Insurance), including Module CA (Capital Adequacy) and Module CI (Captive Insurance), issued under the Central Bank of Bahrain and Financial Institutions Law 2006

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

Central Bank of Bahrain and Financial Institutions Law 2006. Module CA is the CBB directive on capital adequacy of insurance licensees issued under Article 38 of that law. The directive was adopted in January 2007 and the module records a later amendment in January 2011. Category C1 and Category C2, and their minimum funds, are CA-2.1.5 and CA-2.1.6, amended January 2007. The Tier 1 exception for captives is CA-1.2.1, amended January 2007 and October 2007. Legal form is AU-2.1.1 to AU-2.1.3, amended July 2007 and, for the special-purpose-vehicle paragraph, January 2008. The annual licensing fee is AU-6.3.3, added July 2007 and amended July 2013. The Insurance Firm Return is BR-1.1, amended January 2007, with the captive risk-gap and reinsurance information in BR-1.1.6. The captive overview in ES-1.17.3 records the exemptions from quarterly and group financial reporting. The Bank's insurance page states that the CBB has regulated insurance since 2002. Central Bank of Bahrain correspondence, November 2026, refers to the Authorisation Module (AU) of CBB Rulebook Volume 3 (Insurance), May 2026 update, for the applicable licensing requirements, and to the CBB Licensing Directory for institutions currently licensed.

Licensing Speed
After receipt of the required forms and supporting documents, the Central Bank of Bahrain assesses the application and the proposed business model before determining next steps (Central Bank of Bahrain correspondence, November 2026). That correspondence does not state a processing timeline.
Premium Tax Rate
No premium tax is imposed by the captive licensing-fee rule. Value-added tax is 10 percent on taxable insurance other than exempt life insurance and life reinsurance. A 15 percent domestic minimum top-up tax applies from 1 January 2025 to in-scope multinational enterprises with annual global revenue of at least EUR 750 million. A proposed 10 percent tax on specified local-company profits was described by the National Bureau for Revenue on 30 December 2025 as a draft for the legislature, aimed at 2027, and is not stated as a tax in force.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Central Bank of Bahrain correspondence, November 2026, states that the captive licence application fee is BD 100, non-refundable, and payable on submission through the CBB online application portal (https://www.cbb.gov.bh/application-for-a-license/). The annual licensing fee on grant is the fixed captive fee in AU-6.3.3. The rulebook states that Ministry of Commerce and Industry incorporation capital for a Bahraini special purpose vehicle is BD 1,000. That figure is an incorporation-capital floor, not the CBB licensing fee, and the captive must still meet the Module CA minimum fund.
Annual Maintenance Fee
BD 1,000 fixed annual licensing fee for a captive insurance firm (AU-6.3.3, amended July 2013). An insurance firm that is not a captive pays a fixed annual licensing fee of BD 6,000. The Bank's licensing page publishes the same captive fee of BD 1,000.
Annual Filing Requirements
An insurance firm prepares and submits an Insurance Firm Return for each financial year within 3 months of the financial year end (BR-1.1.1 and BR-1.1.5). The financial year is 12 months ending on 31 December unless the CBB consents in writing to another period or year end. The period cannot be shorter than 6 months or longer than 18 months (BR-1.1.2). Captive insurers submit additional return information on the risk gap, being the degree to which potential liabilities under the policy limits exceed available assets and the resources available to meet that gap, and on reinsurance providers, limits and claims (BR-1.1.6). The return is submitted on the prescribed form, with an actuary's certificate and report where applicable (BR-1.1.5). It is reviewed under the international standard on related services for agreed-upon procedures, and the review certifies that the information agrees with the books and records and with the audited financial statements (BR-1.1.14). The external auditor's report, in the CBB format, is provided within 4 months of the financial year end (BR-1.1.16). ES-1.17.3 records that captives are exempt from quarterly and group financial reporting and that public-disclosure requirements do not apply to them. The sections reviewed do not state an in-Bahrain board-meeting requirement or a periodic on-site examination interval.
