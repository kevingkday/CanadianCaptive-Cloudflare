Guam

Guam licenses captive insurance companies under Chapter 23 of Title 22 of the Guam Code Annotated. The Compiler of Laws source note says the chapter was added by Public Law 17-85 and later amended, including by Public Law 24-104 and by Public Law 27-54, which provided for protected cell companies. The Commissioner of Banking and Insurance, in the Department of Revenue and Taxation, is the licensing authority. A pure captive may insure only its parent and affiliated entities. A group captive may insure only its member organizations and their affiliates. An industrial insured captive may insure the industrial insureds in its group, their affiliates, and controlled unaffiliated businesses. An industrial insured uses an employee insurance manager or buyer, pays at least $15,000 of aggregate annual premium, and has at least 10 full-time employees. Minimum paid-in capital is $50,000 for a pure captive, $100,000 for a stock group captive, and $150,000 for a stock industrial insured captive. A rent-a-captive or protected cell captive starts at $150,000 for the first client or cell and rises by $150,000 for each additional client or cell, to a maximum of $750,000. Free surplus is $100,000 for a pure captive and $250,000 for a rent-a-captive or protected cell company. A captive that holds a Special Qualifying Certificate pays no Guam tax on premium or other insurance income except ad valorem tax on property used to produce income, and it pays its Guam income tax and then receives a full rebate under the certificate. Chapter 58 of Title 12 separately allows a 100 percent income-tax rebate on insurance underwriting income, and a business-privilege-tax abatement on insurance underwriting receipts, each for up to 20 years and renewable. Public Law 24-266, signed on 17 August 1998, amended that rebate section. It is not the captive licensing statute. The published captive regulation still states a $200 application fee and a $2,500 licence fee. The Compiler's chapter and the Department pages reviewed do not publish a captive census or a captive-unit headcount.

Tax Environment
Section 23113 applies only to a captive that has been issued a Special Qualifying Certificate under 12 GCA Chapter 58. That captive pays no tax to the government of Guam on its premium or other insurance income, except ad valorem taxes on real and personal property used in the production of income. To the extent it must first pay its Guam income tax on that income, the tax is rebated in full under the certificate. Section 58128.4 allows a rebate of 100 percent of income tax paid on income from insurance underwriting inside or outside Guam, including income from investing funds derived from that underwriting, for up to 20 consecutive years, renewable for further periods of 20 years. For Guam risks and Guam policyholders, the beneficiary may not issue a policy directly to the policyholder and may issue it only through a licensed Guam insurance broker. Section 58127.5 abates business privilege tax, for up to 20 years and renewable for further periods of 20 years, on gross receipts from underwriting insurance risks in or out of Guam and on gross receipts from investing funds generated by that underwriting. The ordinary Qualifying Certificate income-tax rebate in section 58128.1 is up to 75 percent. The 100 percent insurance rebate is the specific rule. Section 58104(k) says a Qualifying Certificate may be issued for insurance issuance by a commercial insurer, reinsurer, or captive insurer that meets Title 22, Chapter 15, Article 3. Neither Chapter 23 nor Chapter 58 states that every captive receives the rebate without a certificate.

Free surplus of not less than $250,000 (section 23105(6)). A protected cell is not a separate legal person (section 23106).
Form of capital and surplus Section 23104 requires the capital to be cash deposited in a Federal Reserve member bank licensed to do business in Guam and approved by the Commissioner. Section 23105 allows surplus to be cash, or an irrevocable letter of credit issued by a Federal Reserve member bank and approved by the Commissioner, or any other form acceptable to the Commissioner. The published captive regulation also accepts an irrevocable letter of credit, from a Guam-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank approved by the Commissioner, as proof of compliance with both sections 23104 and 23105.

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

22 GCA Chapter 23, sections 23101 to 23120, in the Compiler of Laws text. The source note says section 23101 was added by Public Law 17-85 and amended by Public Law 24-104, Public Law 27-54, and Public Law 27-88. Public Law 27-54 provided for protected cell companies. Capital and surplus are sections 23104 and 23105. Reports are section 23107. Examinations are section 23108. The premium and income-tax rule for a captive holding a Special Qualifying Certificate is section 23113, amended by Public Law 23-109 and Public Law 27-54. Chapter 16 of the captive insurance regulations published by the Compiler, citing rule-making authority in 22 GCA section 23101, sets the application, licence, and renewal fees. 12 GCA Chapter 58: section 58104(k) lists captive insurers among Qualifying Certificate applicants; section 58127.5 abates business privilege tax on insurance underwriting receipts; section 58128.4, added by Public Law 23-109 on 26 July 1996 and amended by Public Law 24-266 on 17 August 1998, provides the 100 percent income-tax rebate. The Compiler's source note for Chapter 23 does not print a calendar year for Public Law 17-85, and that public law was not separately retrieved.

Licensing Speed
Chapter 23 does not set a number of days in which the Commissioner must grant or refuse a licence. Section 23102(f) says that if the Commissioner is satisfied the filings comply, the Commissioner may grant a licence. Section 23118 requires the Guam Captive Insurance Advisory Committee to recommend, within 30 days of receiving the information, whether the applicant should be licensed. The recommendation is advisory. The published regulation requires the $2,500 licence fee within 30 days after approval of an incorporated applicant, or within 60 days after approval of an applicant that still has to incorporate.
Premium Tax Rate
A captive holding a Special Qualifying Certificate pays no Guam tax on premium or other insurance income except ad valorem property tax, and its Guam income tax on that income is paid and then rebated in full (22 GCA section 23113). Section 58128.4 allows a 100 percent income-tax rebate on insurance underwriting income for up to 20 years, renewable. Section 58127.5 abates business privilege tax on those underwriting receipts for up to 20 years, renewable. The rebate and abatement are certificate benefits. They are not stated as automatic for every captive.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
Section 23102(d) requires a non-refundable fee of $200 for examining, investigating, and processing the licence application. The captive regulation published by the Compiler repeats that $200 statutory application fee. Within 30 days after the Commissioner notifies an already incorporated applicant of approval, the regulation requires payment of a $2,500 licence fee. An applicant that is not yet incorporated pays the same $2,500 within 60 days after notice of approval, together with the incorporation filings. The statute does not state the $2,500 amount. Corporate filing fees of the Department are not set in Chapter 23.
Annual Maintenance Fee
The published regulation says every licence expires on the 1 April following issuance. A captive that wants to renew applies on or before 1 March. The renewal is a letter identifying the company, evidence that it still meets sections 23104 and 23105, and a check for the $2,500 annual licence fee. The renewal is deemed granted unless the Commissioner rejects it in writing. Chapter 23 does not itself state an annual dollar fee. No later fee schedule changing the $200 or $2,500 amounts was retrieved.
Annual Filing Requirements
Section 23107(a) requires a pure captive to submit a statement of financial condition, written on generally accepted accounting principles and audited by an independent certified public accountant, on or before the last day of the sixth month after the end of its fiscal year. A captive that is not a pure captive files, on or before 1 March, a National Association of Insurance Commissioners annual statement as of the preceding 31 December, verified by at least two principal officers, and a risk-based capital report. Its audit is due on or before 1 June. The statements include reserves for known claims and expenses, claims incurred but not reported and expenses, unearned premiums, and bad debts. An actuarial opinion on known-claim and incurred-but-not-reported reserves is included in the audited statements. For a captive other than a pure captive, that opinion is filed with the annual statement by 1 March. A late filing may be fined up to $500 a day and is a ground for suspension, revocation, or non-renewal. The Commissioner examines each captive at least once every three years, and may extend that period to five years if a comprehensive annual audit of satisfactory scope is performed by independent auditors the Commissioner approves. The captive pays the examination cost. At least one of not fewer than three incorporators must be a Guam resident. The captive maintains its principal place of business on Guam and appoints a resident registered agent. The published regulation still says every licensed captive files a National Association of Insurance Commissioners statement by 1 March and an audit by the end of the sixth month. Section 23107, as amended by Public Law 24-104 and Public Law 27-54, is the later filing rule for a pure captive. The Department's 4 February 2026 financial-statement letter is addressed to domestic and alien insurers under other sections of Title 22. Section 23115 says provisions of Title 22 outside Chapter 23 do not apply to captives except where Chapter 23 specifically refers to them.
