Nevada

Nevada licenses captive insurers under NRS Chapter 694C, added in 1999 and administered by the Captive Section of the Nevada Division of Insurance. A captive may be a pure, association, agency, rental, or sponsored captive, an alien or branch captive, or a state-chartered risk retention group, and a sponsored captive may establish protected cells. The Division's captive homepage states that Nevada offers a series structure that lets sponsored captives create protected cells with independent corporate structures. A white paper on protected cells and the series LLC is listed on the forms page as not yet published, and Chapter 694C itself authorizes protected cells rather than using the term series LLC. The Division's 2025 Insurance Market Report states that, as of October 2024, Nevada had 147 captive insurers and 135 sponsored captive cells, and that Nevada ranked 10th as a U.S. domicile. That report does not publish a type split, a formation count, or a surrender count with those figures. A separate sentence in the same report refers to approximately 91 captives and 160 cells; that sentence is approximate and does not match the dated census, so it is not used here. Unimpaired paid-in capital and unencumbered surplus are at least $200,000 for a pure captive, $500,000 for an association captive, a sponsored captive, or a state-chartered risk retention group, $600,000 for an agency captive, and $800,000 for a rental captive. A dormant captive, a status added in 2019, maintains at least $25,000. Direct premium tax is 0.4 percent, 0.2 percent, and 0.075 percent on successive $20 million bands. Assumed-reinsurance premium tax under NRS 694C.450 is 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.025 percent on the same bands. The Division's annual-filings page prints 0.255 percent on the first reinsurance band; the statute and the application-fees page both state 0.225 percent, and this profile uses 0.225 percent. The annual minimum tax is $5,000 and the annual maximum is $175,000. For a sponsored captive the maximum applies to each protected cell and not to the sponsor as a whole. Nevada has no state income tax. The nonrefundable application fee is $500, license issuance and annual renewal are $300, and NRS 680C.110 adds a $250 initial fee and a $250 annual fee for a captive insurer. The Division states the combined application charge as $1,050 and the annual renewal as $550, due by March 1. A captive licensed after 1 July 2003 receives a one-time nonrefundable $5,000 credit against the first year in which premium tax is owed. The board meets at least once each year in Nevada. The contact page names Michael Reynolds, Management Analyst, as captive staff and does not give him a program-head title or publish a section headcount. No standard licensing turnaround is published.

Tax Environment
NRS 694C.450 imposes premium tax due not later than 1 March. Net direct premiums are taxed at two-fifths of 1 percent on the first $20 million, one-fifth of 1 percent on the next $20 million, and seventy-five thousandths of 1 percent thereafter. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.025 percent on those same bands. Reinsurance tax is not levied on risks already taxed as direct premiums, and it is not levied on a qualifying assumption of loss reserves from an insurer under common ownership and control that is being discontinued. If the calculated tax is less than $5,000, the tax is $5,000. The maximum aggregate tax is $175,000. For a sponsored captive that maximum applies to each protected cell and does not apply to the sponsored captive as a whole. Two or more captives under common ownership and control, defined as 80 percent ownership, are taxed as one captive. The premium tax is in lieu of other Nevada occupation and local taxes, except the taxes in NRS Chapters 363A, 363B, and 363C and ad valorem taxes on real or personal property in Nevada used to produce income. A captive licensed after 1 July 2003 receives a nonrefundable $5,000 credit against the first year in which it owes the tax, and not more than once. A dormant captive is not liable for the tax while the certificate of dormancy is in force. Branch tax applies only to branch business. The Division's FAQ states that Nevada has no state income tax. The Division's annual-filings page prints a 0.255 percent rate on the first $20 million of reinsurance premiums. NRS 694C.450(2)(a) and the application-fees page state 0.225 percent, and the 0.225 percent rate is used here.

State-chartered risk retention group Not less than $500,000 (NRS 694C.250(1)(f)).
Dormant captive Not less than $25,000 of unimpaired paid-in capital and surplus while a certificate of dormancy is in force (NRS 694C.259(3)(a)).
Form of capital Cash or an irrevocable letter of credit, unless the Commissioner prescribes otherwise. A letter of credit must be issued by a Nevada-chartered bank or a Federal Reserve member bank the Commissioner approves, and it must renew automatically unless the issuer gives 90 days' notice. A Commissioner-approved surplus note may also evidence capital and surplus. The Commissioner may require additional capital based on the type, volume, and nature of the business (NRS 694C.250).

Regulatory Framework

Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 694C (Captive Insurers) and Nevada Administrative Code Chapter 694C

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

NRS Chapter 694C, added in 1999. Capital and surplus are NRS 694C.250. The certificate of dormancy was added in 2019 as NRS 694C.259 and amended in 2021. Application and license fees are NRS 694C.220 and 694C.230, plus the captive-insurer fee in NRS 680C.110. The annual report is NRS 694C.400. Examinations are NRS 694C.410. Premium tax is NRS 694C.450, amended in 2025, and branch tax is NRS 694C.455. Sponsored captives and protected cells are NRS 694C.195. Authorized insurance is NRS 694C.300, also amended in 2025. Annual-report and audit detail is also in NAC Chapter 694C.

Premium Tax Rate
Direct premiums: 0.4 percent on the first $20 million, 0.2 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.075 percent thereafter. Assumed reinsurance premiums: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.025 percent on the same bands. Annual minimum $5,000. Annual maximum $175,000, applied per protected cell for a sponsored captive. Due 1 March. One-time $5,000 credit in the first year tax is owed, for a captive licensed after 1 July 2003.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
NRS 694C.220 requires a nonrefundable application fee of $500. NRS 694C.230 requires $300 for issuance of the license. NRS 680C.110 requires a $250 initial fee for a captive insurer. The Division's annual-filings page states that those three amounts are collected together as a $1,050 application fee. Outside legal, financial, and examination services used to review the application are paid by the applicant. The Division's application-fees page also lists an incorporation fee of $75 minimum. Secretary of State formation fees are separate from the Division's $1,050 figure.
Annual Maintenance Fee
NRS 694C.230 requires an annual license fee of $300 on or before 1 March, plus the fees required by NRS 680C.110. NRS 680C.110 sets the captive-insurer annual fee at $250. The Division states the combined annual license renewal as $550, due by 1 March. A dormant captive continues to pay the annual fee and the NRS 680C.110 renewal fees.
Annual Filing Requirements
By 1 March, a captive files its premium-tax return, its business plan, and the $550 annual renewal. By 30 June, a captive other than a state-chartered risk retention group files a GAAP financial-condition report, with modifications the Commissioner accepts, an audited financial statement certified by an independent certified public accountant, and an actuarial opinion. A pure captive may be allowed to file the annual report within 60 days after its parent's fiscal year-end, and it still files the premium-tax support by 1 March. A state-chartered risk retention group files on the schedule in NRS 680A.270 and files its financial statement under NRS 680A.265. A late financial report, audit, or risk-retention-group statement draws $100 a day, up to $3,000. A sponsored captive's 30 June report includes the financial experience of each protected cell. Filings are uploaded through the Division's financial-filing portal. A dormant captive does not file the NRS 694C.400 annual report, an audit, or an actuarial opinion while the certificate is in force. The certificate of dormancy is renewed after five years. Examinations are at least every three years, or every five years if the Commissioner accepts comprehensive annual audits by an appointed independent auditor. That cycle does not apply to a pure captive, which the Commissioner may examine at any reasonable time. The examination is at the captive's cost. The board meets at least once each year in Nevada, the principal place of business is in Nevada, and at least one director, or one member of a reciprocal's subscribers' advisory committee or its attorney-in-fact, is a Nevada resident.
