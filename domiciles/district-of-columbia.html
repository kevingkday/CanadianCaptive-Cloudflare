District of Columbia

The District of Columbia licenses captives under the Captive Insurance Company Act of 2004, Chapter 39A of Title 31 of the D.C. Code, administered by the Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking Risk Finance Bureau. The 2000 captive law was repealed and replaced by that Act. The first captive was licensed in 2001. A captive may be a pure, association, agency, rental, branch, or protected-cell captive, and a risk retention group may be licensed as a captive. Any type of captive may form protected cells, including incorporated cells that are separate legal entities. Special purpose financial captives are authorized in Subchapter II. A wildcard provision lets the Commissioner authorize a qualified captive to engage in any activity permitted for a captive in any other jurisdiction. On 30 October 2025 the Bureau reported 215 active risk-bearing entities: 49 pure, including 6 protected-cell-captive cores; 10 association captives that are not risk retention groups, including 3 protected-cell-captive cores; 1 agency protected-cell captive; 4 branch captives; 16 rental captives, all protected-cell captives; 29 risk retention groups; and 106 protected cells, of which 78 were unincorporated and 28 were incorporated. Through that date in 2025 the Bureau had licensed 23 entities: 1 incorporated cell, 20 unincorporated protected cells, and 2 risk retention groups. It also said 413 captives had been licensed since 2001. The same presentation reported 2024 captive assets of $11,396,881,785 and 2024 gross written premium of $2,954,868,515. DISB also posts a licensed-captives roster dated 14 July 2026. That roster does not state a total, so the 30 October 2025 census is the figure stored here. A captive other than a protected-cell captive maintains at least $100,000 of unimpaired capital. Unencumbered surplus is at least $150,000 for a pure captive, $300,000 for an agency captive, a rental captive, or a stock association captive, and $500,000 for a mutual or reciprocal association captive. The Commissioner sets cell capital and surplus, and the Bureau's 30 October 2025 presentation states that the minimum is $0 for a protected-cell captive and $0 for cells, including incorporated cells. Non-risk-retention-group direct premium tax starts at 0.25 percent on the first $25 million, with a $7,500 minimum and a $100,000 maximum. The application review fee is $500 and the certificate-of-authority fee is $300, renewed annually by 30 April. Properly documented applications are processed within 30 days. The Bureau page still displays Dana Sheppard as Associate Commissioner for Risk Finance. The 30 October 2025 presentation lists her as Deputy Commissioner for Market Operations and lists Sean O'Donnell as Associate Commissioner, and the Bureau's 7 November 2025 and 17 December 2025 notices are issued by Sean O'Donnell in that office. No dormant-captive statute is in force. The October 2025 presentation said a proposal to add one had been submitted and that no significant captive law had passed in 2025.

Tax Environment
Section 31-3931.12 requires premium tax by 2 March. For a captive that is not a risk retention group, net direct premiums are taxed at 0.250 percent on the first $25 million, 0.150 percent on the next $25 million, and 0.050 percent thereafter. A risk retention group pays 0.38 percent on the first $20 million of total net direct premiums, 0.25 percent on the next $20 million, and 0.18 percent thereafter. Assumed-reinsurance premiums are taxed at 0.225 percent on the first $25 million, 0.150 percent on the next $25 million, and 0.025 percent thereafter. Reinsurance tax is not levied on risks already taxed as direct premiums, or on a qualifying assumption of loss reserves from an insurer under common ownership and control that is being discontinued. A captive other than a risk retention group licensed as an association captive pays at least $7,500. A risk retention group licensed as an association captive pays at least $15,000. The total tax does not exceed $100,000 in any year. Annuity business is not taxed. Branch tax applies only to branch business. A captive licensed in the last quarter of the calendar year may ask the Commissioner to reduce the minimum. The tax is in lieu of other District taxes except real property tax and personal property tax. The Bureau's 2025 reporting instructions say each protected cell captive and each incorporated cell files a separate premium-tax return, incorporated cells are subject to the $7,500 minimum, unincorporated protected cells file a consolidated return with the protected cell captive, and each unincorporated protected cell is not subject to the minimum. From 1 January 2026, premium tax and certificate-of-authority renewal payments are made through NAIC OPTins. The NAIC charges $17.50 per OPTins transaction.

Rental captive At least $300,000 of unencumbered surplus (section 31-3931.06(f)(4)).
Association captive, mutual or reciprocal At least $500,000 of unencumbered surplus (section 31-3931.06(f)(5)).
Protected cell captive and each protected cell The Commissioner sets the minimum capital and the minimum surplus for the protected cell captive and for each cell. Protected-cell-captive capital may be zero if each cell maintains the capital required for that cell. A protected cell captive that does not write insurance business in the District is not required to maintain surplus if each cell maintains the surplus required for that cell (section 31-3931.06(a)(2) and (f)(6)). The Bureau's 30 October 2025 presentation states the minimum as $0 for protected cell captives and $0 for cells, including incorporated cells.
Special purpose financial captive At least $100,000 of unimpaired capital and at least $150,000 of unencumbered surplus (section 31-3932.05(a) and (e)).
Risk retention group Section 31-3931.06(l) applies section 31-2502.12 to a risk retention group licensed as a captive. The captive surplus schedule does not state a separate risk-retention-group dollar floor, and no separate floor is restated here.
Form of capital and surplus Cash or an irrevocable letter of credit, unless the Commissioner provides otherwise. The letter of credit is issued by a District-chartered bank, or a District branch of a Federal Reserve member bank or an FDIC-insured bank, on a form the Commissioner approves, and it renews automatically each year.

Regulatory Framework

Captive Insurance Company Act of 2004, D.C. Code Title 31, Chapter 39A

Applicable Statutes & Regulations

D.C. Code Title 31, Chapter 39A, Subchapter I, enacted as D.C. Law 15-262 (17 March 2005) and amended since, including the Captive Insurance Company Amendment Act of 2014, D.C. Law 20-203. Chapter 39, the 2000 captive law, is repealed by section 31-3931.23. Capital and surplus are section 31-3931.06. Protected cells are section 31-3931.04. Premium tax is section 31-3931.12. The annual report is section 31-3931.13. Financial examination is section 31-3931.14. Certificate-of-authority renewal by 30 April is section 31-3931.09(d), as cited in the Bureau's 2025 reporting instructions. Special purpose financial captive capital is section 31-3932.05. Non-risk-retention-group audit timing is also in 26 DCMR section 3702, as cited by the Bureau.

Licensing Speed
DISB will process a properly documented application within 30 days. The forming page states that standard, and the Bureau's 30 October 2025 presentation states licensing of 30 days or less. The one-pager also states that many business-plan changes are approved within 48 hours.
Premium Tax Rate
Non-risk-retention-group direct premiums: 0.25 percent on the first $25 million, 0.15 percent on the next $25 million, and 0.05 percent thereafter. Risk-retention-group direct premiums: 0.38 percent, 0.25 percent, and 0.18 percent on successive $20 million bands. Assumed reinsurance: 0.225 percent, 0.150 percent, and 0.025 percent on successive $25 million bands. Minimum $7,500, or $15,000 for a risk retention group licensed as an association captive. Maximum $100,000. Due 2 March. Incorporated cells owe the $7,500 minimum. An unincorporated protected cell is not separately subject to the minimum.
Incorporation / Initial Fee
The forming page and the Bureau's 30 October 2025 presentation require a $500 application review fee and a $300 certificate-of-authority fee with the application. Incorporation is handled inside the Department. The presentation states that there are no fees other than the $500 application fee, the $300 annual certificate-of-authority fee, and annual premium taxes. Section 31-3931.04(b)(4) also says a protected cell captive pays an additional annual fee for each protected cell in an amount the Commissioner establishes. No separate dollar amount for that cell fee was published on the pages reviewed. A certification of captive documents is $10, paid in advance, under the Bureau's certification-fee notice.
Annual Maintenance Fee
Annual certificate-of-authority renewal fee of $300, due on or before 30 April under section 31-3931.09(d), as stated in the 17 December 2025 reporting instructions. Beginning with the 2026-2027 renewals, renewal payments are made through NAIC OPTins. The NAIC charges $17.50 per transaction. The Commissioner may fine, suspend, or revoke a certificate that is not renewed by 1 August.
Annual Filing Requirements
On or before 2 March, a captive, including each protected cell, submits a report of its financial condition prepared by a certified public accountant. Reporting uses generally accepted accounting principles unless the Commissioner accepts modifications. A protected cell captive files a consolidated report for its cells, and the 2025 instructions also require separate unaudited DC blanks for the protected cell captive, each incorporated cell, and each unincorporated protected cell. A pure captive may be allowed to report on its parent's fiscal year, with the report due within 60 days after fiscal year-end, and it still files premium-tax support by 2 March. Risk retention groups also comply with Chapter 3 of Title 31 and file the actuarial opinion by 2 March and the actuarial opinion summary by 15 March. Non-risk-retention-group audited financial reports are due 30 June under 26 DCMR section 3702. Risk-retention-group audits are due 1 June. Combined cell audits and combined actuarial opinions, when used, are due 30 June. A branch captive files copies of the reports its parent files at home, certified by two officers, plus a branch premium and reserve statement. For a 31 December branch, those filings are due 30 June. Premium tax is due 2 March. The certificate of authority is renewed by 30 April. Financial filings are emailed to captive.filings@dc.gov. From 1 January 2026, premium tax and renewal payments go through OPTins. The board meets at least once each year in the District. The Bureau's one-pager states that the meeting may be held by teleconference or webcast and that a local director is not required. The captive maintains an office in the District. Examinations are at the Commissioner's discretion and at the captive's cost. The Commissioner may waive the five-year examination in Chapter 14 for a captive that is not a risk retention group, has filed unqualified audits, has sufficient surplus, and is in compliance. The 30 October 2025 presentation describes non-risk-retention-group supervision as an annual desk analysis, with examinations at the regulator's discretion. No dormant-captive filing regime is in the current statute.
